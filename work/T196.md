title: MM4's end state: the allocator takes frame runs inline and `linked_list_allocator` leaves the kernel
refs: [MM4, L16]
---

MM4 part 2 (2026-11-21) serves small objects (up to 2048 bytes, alignment up to 2048) from `kcore::slab` slabs behind per-hart magazines, with the slabs' frames from a pool of frames that `heap::service` keeps stocked at the safe points under the big lock (`kernel/src/heap.rs`). Everything else still comes from the boot heap (8 MiB of `.bss`) and the grown heap (the window), both `linked_list_allocator`: every request above 2048 bytes, a small request when the pool is empty (a burst of more than 64 frames' worth of small objects between two safe points), and everything before `kmem::init`. KERNEL-V1-SPEC 4.2 wants no boot region beyond a small bump area, no window, whole frame runs from the buddy allocator for large requests, empty slabs returned inline, and the dependency gone (OD138). Those need the allocator to take frames and runs from the frame database with no big lock, which is L16's `FRAME` lock (the allocator runs from interrupt handlers). Proper fix, after L16: a `FRAME` lock below `HEAP`; the allocator takes slab frames and large runs inline (`kcore::slab::run_order` already says what order a request needs); delete the pool, `service_pool`, the window and the boot heap's size (keep a 256 KiB bump region for early boot); remove the dependency; extend `slab_serves_small_objects_and_everything_comes_back` with large blocks and a burst; the T-SPEC churn test for runs. Why acceptable: the slab path is what the common allocation takes, and the old heap is the proven fallback. Risk: a long burst between safe points serves small objects from the old heap (slower, not wrong); the leak oracles count both.
