KT9's host sweep (`ksys/tests/allocfail.rs`) fails every allocation of seventeen scenarios in turn and found, and this slice fixed, thirty-odd infallible allocations in the syscall layer (the registry's and handle table's growth and free lists, the destruction paths, the channel read's result and handle numbers, the socket read, the port prune, the VMO store and committed-page map, the commit's undo list, `vmo_read`'s scratch buffer). Not reached, so not yet known clean: (1) **the process calls** (`process_map` and `process_unmap` insert into the address space's region `BTreeMap`; `process_create` and `process_start` insert into the process table and several per-process maps; `thread_create` and `thread_spawn` push to `thread_objs`, `stacks` and the start table; kill and teardown): they need the process world in the sweep, and, where they use `BTreeMap`, a fallible store like the `PageMap` and the VMO slab; (2) **waits and futexes** (`object_wait`, `port_wait`, `futex_wait`, `channel_call`: their wait-table registrations), which are in `kernel/src/user.rs` until TS3c; (3) the **privileged and boot paths** that still allocate infallibly: `PageMap::from_run` (device and DMA VMOs), `Vmos::create` of the kernel's own VMOs, `start_locked`, the boot image, the program loader's vectors; (3b) the **per-syscall epilogue** in `kernel/src/user.rs`: `cleanup_dead`'s `pending_vmos.push`, `reap_vmos`'s `BTreeSet` and its take and re-push (and `commit_wake`'s wait table), which the harness does not run; the **committed-page map's sparse cost** (`PageMap` sizes its outer vector by the highest page: one page at index 16383 is about 6.6 KiB of uncharged heap, where a `BTreeMap` node was about 200 bytes: charge it, or bound it); the **VMO id generation** wraps after 2^32 uses of one slot (retire the slot at the wrap, as `HandleTable` does, or record why it is safe); (4) **`Registry::sweep`**, which allocates its marking vectors (it runs under pressure, so it should skip rather than fail); (5) **the guest sweep** with a failing kernel allocator (`fault-alloc`), which reaches what the host cannot (the real heap, the scheduler's allocations, the file system and network servers' syscalls). Why acceptable for now: each is outside the calls a program makes in a loop, or privileged, and the sweep now guards the ones it covers against regression. Risk: a program that fills memory can still panic the kernel through them. Proper fix: extend the scenario list as TS3c moves the calls behind `ksys`, convert each reported site, then build the guest half of KT9.

**Findings of the guest sweep on the next scenarios (2026-11-24).** Tried and not kept (the kernel panics on the first allocation that fails): (a) `process_create`: `ksys::create::process_create` makes `Box::new(Process {..})`, `Arc::new(AsCell)`, a `BTreeMap::from`, and `procs.insert` after the charges, all infallible, and `ksys` is `forbid(unsafe_code)`, so it has no fallible `Box`; the proper fix is a `Machine` method that makes the process record from a kernel allocator that can say no (or a fixed slab of process records), plus fallible maps; (b) every wait (`object_wait`, `port_wait`, `futex_wait`): `WaitTable::register*` does `entry().or_default().push()` and a `BTreeMap` insert inside the scheduler's `prepare_block_on` callback, where a failure cannot be reported to the caller; the proper fix is to reserve the registration's room (a per-thread record inside the thread's kernel record, with the object's list linked through it) before the thread decides to block, so registering cannot fail. These two are the next work of KT9's guest half.

## Plan (Fable, 2026-11-23)

A design for the rest of KT9 (this item and T246), from a reading of the code as it is on `bundle4`. Four feature PRs into the epic. Nothing
here changes the lock order or adds a lock class: every new structure is data under a lock that already protects it.

### 0. What the code says, and the rule the plan follows

The first allocation that fails is only the first of many. Reading on from the two findings:

- `process_create` (`ksys/src/create.rs`): `Box::new(Process)`, `Arc::new(AsCell)`, `BTreeMap::from`, `procs.insert`. Stable Rust has no
  fallible `Arc` at all, with or without `unsafe`, so "a Machine method" does not help there; it needs a pool.
- A new thread: `kernel/src/thread.rs` `spawn_with_affinity` is infallible throughout (`kstack::alloc().expect`, `Box<Context>`,
  `sched().spawn_on`, `k.threads.insert`), and its bookkeeping is **sixteen maps keyed by thread or region** (`UserState`: `thread_pid`,
  `calls`, `sched_flags`, `suspends`, `initial_regs`, `exc_pending`, `pending_regs`, `parked_tids`, `interrupts`, `cleaning`, `starts`;
  `Process`: `live_tids`, `thread_objs`, `stacks`, `table_charge`, `charge_end`), about 125 use sites, several of them inserted where nothing
  can be reported (`user_thread_main` registering itself in `kernel/src/user/lifecycle.rs`, `cleaning.insert` in `cleanup_dead`,
  `parked_tids.insert` when a thread parks).
- A wait: after `WaitTable::register*` (`kcore/src/wait.rs`) comes `sleep_until_on`, which inserts into two `BTreeSet`s of timers
  (`kcore/src/sched2.rs` 1109), and every wake is a `VecDeque::push_back` plus a `BTreeMap` insert (`RunQueue::push_back`, 165). **The
  scheduler core allocates on every sleep, wake, spawn and migration.** A port wait also allocates `port_bound_objects`' vector and the two
  watch maps. `kcore::futex::Futexes::enqueue` is a `BTreeMap` of `VecDeque`s.
- The epilogue (`kernel/src/user/wake.rs`): `WaitTable::commit` (`woken`, `fired`, `keep`, a copy of the watching ports, `bound()`), then
  `thread::wake_many` (the scheduler again), `cleanup_dead` (teardown, exception delivery, pager reap, each with vectors).

So every allocation is put in one of three classes, and the sweep's contract is stated per class:

| Class | Where | Mechanism | What the sweep proves |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R, reportable** | before a call's decision | fallible API (`try_reserve`, `FMap`, `FBox`, a pool), `Status::NoMemory`, everything undone | failing it returns NO_MEMORY, state at baseline |
| **P, prepaid** | after the decision, or where nobody can be told | the room was reserved at an R point (thread creation, `port_bind`, object creation); the later step allocates **nothing** | the allocation count of the step is zero (it is simply never reached by N) |
| **N, no-fail** | the scheduler core, teardown and kill, exception delivery, the donation graph, interrupts, heap service | still allocates; runs in `heap::nofail` and draws on a **reserve that class R cannot consume** | not failed by the single-failure sweep; exercised by the persistent mode (section 5) |

Class N is the honest replacement of today's blanket `heap::epilogue`: named, counted, small, with a tidy item per member. Making the
scheduler core allocation-free (intrusive run queues, a timer structure with per-thread nodes, a slot table instead of per-hart maps that
records move between) is a rewrite of `sched2.rs` and its model and shuttle suites; it is **not worth doing inside KT9** and gets its own
item (section 7). Everything else named in T177 and T246 moves to R or P.

### 1. Two small pure tools (kcore, `forbid(unsafe_code)`)

**`kcore::fmap::FMap<K: Ord + Copy, V>`**: an ordered map with fallible growth, the `PageMap` idea with keys: an outer `Vec` of chunks, each
chunk a sorted `Vec<(K, V)>` made once with `try_reserve_exact(CHUNK)` (CHUNK = 16) and never grown. Lookup is two binary searches. An insert
into a full chunk splits it into a spare chunk; a removal merges a chunk into a neighbour when both fit in half a chunk (so the chunk count
stays within 4n/CHUNK + 1 and removal never allocates). API (the subset of `BTreeMap` the kernel uses): `get`, `get_mut`, `contains_key`,
`remove`, `len`, `iter`, `range`, `first_key_value`, `last_key_value`, `keys`, and

- `reserve(n) -> Result<(), NoMem>` (n <= 4): after `Ok`, the next `n` inserts cannot fail and allocate nothing (it holds `n` spare chunks
  and `n` free outer slots);
- `try_insert(k, v) -> Result<Option<V>, NoMem>` (= `reserve(1)` then insert); `insert_reserved(k, v) -> Option<V>` for the P step, which
  uses only reserved room (a `debug_assert` if there was none; in release it reports through a counter the audits read, never a panic).

`FSet<K>` is `FMap<K, ()>`. Rejected: one sorted vector (an O(n) memmove per thread exit, one large contiguous block) and a real B-tree
(more than is needed: two levels are 1024 outer entries at `MAX_REGIONS` = 16384).

**`kcore::fbox::FBox<T>`**: a fallible box without `unsafe`: `Vec::try_reserve_exact(1)`, push, check `capacity() == 1`,
`into_boxed_slice()`, `Box<[T; 1]>::try_from`. `try_new(v) -> Result<FBox<T>, T>`, `Deref`/`DerefMut`. Used for `Process`, `Context`,
`PendingRegs`, register images.

Tests (host, written first): a random walk of `FMap` against `BTreeMap` (the independent oracle; 300 seeds, with `range` and the chunk
bound checked after every step); a counting allocator that asserts **zero allocations between `reserve(n)` and the n-th insert** and zero in
any `remove`; the failing-allocator child harness of `ksys/tests/allocfail.rs` over every `FMap` operation; `FBox::try_new` makes exactly
one allocation and no reallocation. Mutation checks, once, by hand: a split that allocates instead of taking the spare; a merge that is
skipped (the chunk bound fails).

### 2. Gap (a): processes, regions, threads

**`process_create`.** In order, each step undone by the ones after it failing: the checks and the two charges (as now); the page table;
`procs.reserve(1)`; an address-space cell from a pool; `FBox::try_new(Process {..})` built in an `#[inline(never)]` constructor whose
`table_charge.try_insert(u64::MAX, 1)` is the only allocation inside; `process_object_create_in` (already fallible, already undone);
`procs.insert_reserved`. Every failure is `Status::NoMemory` (SYSCALLS.md already lists it).

- `UserState::procs` becomes `FMap<u64, FBox<Process>>`.
- **The `Arc<AsCell>` pool**: `UserState::as_pool: Vec<Arc<AsCell>>`, filled at boot with `backstops.processes` vacant cells
  (where the kernel builds its `UserState`; 64 today, `AS_HELD_MOST`; at RAM / 1 MiB it is about 200 bytes per MiB of RAM). A boot allocation: failure
  there is a boot failure. `process_create` pops one and arms it through `Arc::get_mut` (`*cell = AsCell::new(pid, aspace)`, safe: the pool's
  reference is the only one). Teardown replaces `AsCell::take(this)` by `AsCell::vacate(&mut Arc<AsCell>) -> AddressSpace` (the same
  uniqueness requirement as `take` has today) and pushes the cell back (the vector's capacity never changes). `klock::aslock` gains
  `AsCell::vacant()` and `vacate`; `kcore::aspace` gains `AddressSpace::vacant()` (no table; never installed). The pool can never be empty
  when the `procs.len() >= backstops.processes` check has passed; if it is, NO_MEMORY. Side benefit: `UserBinding::aspace`, an address
  kept in the thread record, now points at memory that is never freed.
- The boot loader (`kernel/src/user/loader.rs`) uses the same constructor with `expect` (boot path, section 6).

**Regions and per-process maps.** `AddressSpace::regions` (`kcore/src/aspace.rs` 1833) becomes `FMap<u64, Region>`; `map_region` and
`split_at` call `regions.reserve(1)`, and `unmap_range`/`protect_range` `reserve(2)`, **beside the existing `region_limit` check, before
anything is changed**, mapping `NoMem` to `RegionError::TooMany`/`ProtectError::TooMany`'s sibling `NoMemory` (both already reach the caller
as `Status::NoMemory`). `Process::table_charge` and `charge_end` become `FMap` with `try_insert` at their R sites (`ksys/src/thread.rs` 294,
457, 460 and `proc.rs`'s map path), ordered before the mapping they account for so that the undo is the existing one. M9's "minimal cover"
and the `region_ops_model`/`aspace_model` suites are the regression check; they need no change beyond the type.

**Threads: one record per user thread.** The eleven per-thread maps of `UserState` and `Process::thread_objs`/`stacks` collapse into

```
pub struct UThread { pid, obj: Option<ObjId>, stack: Option<SpawnStack>, call: Option<u64>, sched_flags: u8, suspend: Option<Suspend>,
                     initial_regs: Option<FBox<[u64; 32]>>, pending_regs: Option<FBox<PendingRegs>>, exc_pending: Option<u64>,
                     parked: bool, interrupt: bool, cleaning: bool }
UserState::threads: FMap<Tid, UThread>          Process::live_tids: FSet<Tid>   (kept: T191's walk)
```

so a thread's whole later life is field writes (class P, nothing to reserve). The creation protocol, the same for `thread_spawn` and
`process_start` (`kernel/src/user/calls.rs` 230, 290) and the boot loader:

1. R, in `ksys::thread_spawn` / `process_start`, with the other checks: `us.threads.reserve(1)`, `p.live_tids.reserve(1)`,
   `us.waits.reserve_thread()` (section 3), `us.starts.try_insert(token, ThreadStart)` (`starts` stays, as an `FMap`), the register image's
   `FBox` when SUSPENDED (replaces `image_room`, `ksys/src/thread.rs` 214).
2. R, in the kernel: `thread::try_spawn_user(prio, entry, token) -> Option<Tid>`: `kstack::alloc()` (already an `Option`),
   `FBox<Context>`, `heap::reserve_thread()` (section 5); only then the class-N tail (`sched().spawn_on`, `k.threads.insert`). On `None`
   the caller runs the existing undo of `ksys::thread_spawn` (it exists for the later failures already) and returns NO_MEMORY.
3. P, in the same `locked_state` section as the spawn (as `thread_spawn` does today; `process_start` and the loader move their spawn into
   the section too): `threads.insert_reserved(tid, UThread {..})`, `live_tids.insert_reserved`, `waits.admit(tid)`.

`user_thread_main` stops registering itself (the "both orders leave the same state" code in `lifecycle.rs` 24 to 63 and
`thread_registered_by_spawner` go): the spawner is the only registrar, and the thread only reads its record. That is a simplification the
EX1 suite (`suspend_model.rs`, `spawn_calls.rs`, the SUSPENDED tests at 1, 2, 4 harts) checks. `thread_write_state`'s `pending_regs` is an
`FBox::try_new` at an R point (NO_MEMORY, add to SYSCALLS.md); `thread_interrupt`, `thread_set_sched`, `channel_call`'s call number, a
park, the cleanup guard are field writes.

### 3. Gap (b): waits

**`WaitTable` allocates only when a thread is admitted.** New shape (`kcore/src/wait.rs`), same meaning:

- `recs: Vec<Rec>` with a free list (`Rec { thread, on: Option<(ObjId, Kind, seq)>, next, prev }`), `idx: FMap<Waiter, u32>`,
  `buckets: Vec<(u32, u32)>` (head and tail of an intrusive doubly linked chain; a power of two, at least the admitted count; an object's
  bucket is its slot index masked), and two scratch vectors `woken: Vec<Waiter>` and `fired: Vec<ObjId>`.
- `reserve_thread() -> Result<(), NoMem>` (R): room for one more record, `idx.reserve(1)`, the bucket array doubled and re-linked when the
  admitted count would pass it (walking the old chains in order keeps each object's waiters in arrival order: the baton of L21b depends on
  it), both scratch vectors to the admitted count. `admit(thread)` (P) and `retire(thread)` (thread end; kernel threads that wait are
  admitted where they are created, at boot).
- `register`, `register_call`, `register_poller`, `forget`: link or unlink the thread's own record at the tail of its object's bucket.
  **No allocation, so nothing to report from inside `wait_deadline_registered`** (`kernel/src/thread.rs` 1427), which stays as it is.
- `commit`: releases by unlinking in place while walking the chain (no `keep`), pushes to the `woken` scratch (at most one entry per
  registered thread) and returns it as a slice or a taken-and-recycled vector like `take_dirty`/`recycle_dirty`; `fired` records only
  objects that had a registration (at most one per registered thread), which is all the baton needs; the overflow path walks the buckets.
- **The port watch moves to bind time.** `watchers`/`watching`/`watch_port` and the `bound` vectors go. `kcore::ipc` keeps the reverse index
  permanently: `Kobjs::bound_by: FMap<(ObjId, ObjId), u32>` (object, port) to a count, `try_insert` in `port_bind` (`ipc.rs` 1146, already
  an R point with a `try_reserve`), removed at unbind, prune, `end_bindings_made_through` and port destruction (removal never allocates).
  `commit` takes `binders: impl Fn(ObjId, &mut dyn FnMut(ObjId))` and, for a touched object, fires the ports that bind it; a port with no
  waiter is an empty bucket walk. The `ipc` audit checks the index against the ports' binding lists.

`object_wait`, `port_wait*` and `channel_call` therefore never return NO_MEMORY for the registration. C3b and PK1 are untouched: prepare,
register, sleep are the same steps in the same section; only the table's inside changes. `kcore/tests/wait_model.rs` is the oracle (its
reference model does not know the representation); it gains admit/retire steps, a rehash in the middle of a walk, and the zero-allocation
assertion on register, forget and commit. Its three mutations (wake-all, any-message, handles ignored) are re-run once, plus one new: a
rehash that reverses a chain (the baton test must fail).

**`futex_wait`** is an R point (it decides to sleep after the enqueue): `Futexes::enqueue` becomes fallible (`FMap` plus
`VecDeque::try_reserve`), `ksys::futex_wait_enqueue` returns `FutexCheck::Done(NoMemory)`, on both the full and the quiet path; SYSCALLS.md
gains NO_MEMORY for `futex_wait`, and `rt`'s futex users treat it as a spurious return (check each loop). `Futexes::wake` fills a
fixed array of 16 and the caller wakes batch by batch (no vector). PI futexes, the donation graph and pager waits are class N for now
(section 7).

### 4. T246: the epilogue

With section 3 the commit itself is allocation-free. The rest of `notify_held`:

- `thread::wake_many`: the scheduler, class N.
- `cleanup_dead_held`: `cleaning` is a `UThread` field; `drain_dead`/`recycle` are already prepaid; the arms that end a process, resolve an
  exception or destroy a pager run in `heap::nofail` (class N: a teardown cannot be refused).
- `ksys::pager::reap_closed_channels`: two vectors on every call when a pager exists; make it a cursor over `pager_ends` that destroys one
  pager per pass and wakes from a prepaid buffer in `Pagers` (reserved in `pager_create`, an R point).
- `cow_sync` and `AsLocks::all` (`kernel/src/user/aslocks.rs` 56): the two vectors are taken from one scratch pair reserved at boot to
  `backstops.processes` and put back by `Drop` (the object lock is not reentrant, so one pair is enough). `AsLocks::all` no longer fails,
  and the "caller's own space only" fallback of `cow_sync` goes. (T214 removes `all` altogether later.)
- `heap::epilogue` and `faultalloc::exempt` are deleted. The blanket exemption is replaced by the class-N predicate of section 5.

### 5. Class N: `heap::nofail` and the reserve (kernel/src/heap.rs)

- **Predicate.** An allocation is class N when the hart is in an interrupt handler, holds a `TT` or `RQ` lock (the order checker's held
  stack already sees every allocation through `bkl::check_use(Class::Heap)`, so the scheduler needs no call-site marker), or is inside
  `heap::nofail(|| ..)` (a per-hart depth in `PerHart`). Everything else is class R.
- **Reserve.** Class R is refused (null) when serving it from the free-list heaps would leave `boot.free() + grown.free()` below
  `RESERVE = 1 MiB + 256 bytes * live user threads`; slab allocations that the pool can serve are not gated (they do not touch the last
  resort). `heap::reserve_thread() -> bool` / `unreserve_thread()` move the figure at thread creation (R) and end. `LOW_WATER` becomes
  `4 MiB + RESERVE` so growth still leads. Reason for the per-thread term: class N memory after exhaustion is bounded by the population
  that exists (queue, timer and map entries per thread), and the population cannot grow once class R is refused.
- **`fault-alloc`** fails only class-R allocations of the armed thread (so a tick that lands in a scenario no longer shifts N), and counts
  the bytes class N allocates per call; `debug_stat` 207 returns the peak. A second mode (`debug_stat` 208, as `PERSIST` on the host):
  every class-R allocation from the N-th on fails, which is what real exhaustion looks like with the reserve in force.
- `scripts/check_docs.py` gets a count check of `heap::nofail(` sites per file, like `check_atomics`: the class cannot grow silently.
- Lock order: the gate reads the hart's own state and the two free counts inside the `HEAP` section the allocator is already in; one more
  atomic in `heap.rs` (update `check_atomics`' count). INVARIANTS.md K6 gains the reserve sentence; no class is added or moved.

### 6. The smaller T177 sub-items

- **`Registry::sweep`** (`kcore/src/object.rs` 671 to 858). Marking: `mark_work` is prepaid, `try_reserve`d to the slot count where
  `insert` grows the slot table (an object is pushed at most once per epoch, so it cannot overflow: 16 bytes per slot); `Object::held(&mut
  Vec)` becomes `for_each_held(&mut dyn FnMut(ObjId))` (as `for_each_watched` already is), with the object taken out of its slot for the
  visit and put back, so the `scan` buffer goes. `shade` keeps a `debug_assert` and a **fail-safe**: if a push ever had no room it sets
  `mark_incomplete`, and a sweep with that flag set frees nothing. Freeing: before `doom_slot`, count the object's references with the
  visitor and `try_reserve` `dropped_refs`, `peers`, `credits`, `free` (both scratch vectors become fields); if any fails the sweep
  **is abandoned between objects** (phase Idle, threshold unchanged, `Done(swept so far)`): what was doomed is fully processed, the rest
  waits for the next sweep. `UserState::sweep_start`'s `root_scan` collect becomes a `try_reserve` (no memory: no sweep starts);
  `UserState::roots()` likewise for the whole-sweep callers. SW1 is unchanged. Tests: `sweep_slices.rs` under the failing allocator (every
  N: no reachable object freed, audits hold, a later sweep with memory frees the garbage); mutation, once: `shade` dropping a push without
  the flag must be caught by the existing "reachable object freed" check.
- **`PageMap`'s sparse cost: not worth a mechanism.** `MAX_VMO_PAGES` is 16384, so the outer vector is at most 256 entries (6.1 KiB) per
  VMO, and VMOs are bounded by the job's object quota. Record that bound in the module comment and pin it with
  `const _: () = assert!(MAX_VMO_PAGES / CHUNK * 24 <= 8192)`: raising the VMO size limit then fails the build until the cost is charged
  (KERNEL-V1-SPEC 4.3's per-VMO page index is the moment).
- **VMO id generation: already done.** `kcore/src/aspace.rs` 346 to 351 retires a slot whose 32-bit generation is exhausted (CNT1,
  `counters_cnt1.rs`). Strike it from the item.
- **Privileged and boot paths.** `PageMap::from_run` becomes fallible (ten lines; device and DMA VMO creation is a syscall, if a privileged
  one) and its callers return NO_MEMORY. The kernel's own VMOs, `start_locked`, the boot image and the loader's vectors run once at boot
  with nobody to report to: **allocation failure there is a boot failure by design**; keep their `// INVARIANT:` allows and say so once in
  TEST-TOOLS.md. Not worth more.

### 7. What to record instead of doing (new items, filed by the PR that introduces class N)

- `kind: tidy`, long: **the scheduler core allocates** on sleep, wake, spawn and migration (`sched2.rs`: `RunQueue`, `timers`,
  `hard_timers`, `demoted`, the per-hart `threads` maps; `kernel/src/thread.rs`: `Kernel::threads`, `queues`). Acceptable: class N with
  a per-thread reserve; risk: a fragmented last-resort heap cannot serve a large `VecDeque` growth (one priority level with tens of
  thousands of threads). Proper fix: per-thread nodes made at spawn (intrusive queues, a timer heap of slots); when: the next time
  `Sched2` is opened, or FZ1 review.
- `kind: tidy`, long: **class N outside the scheduler**: teardown and kill, exception delivery (`ksys/src/exception.rs`), the donation
  graph (`kcore::donate`), PI futexes (`kcore::pifutex`: four maps), pager waits (`kcore::pager`). Each could be prepaid from the thread
  record once `UThread` exists; none is in KT9's scenario list.
- T246 is closed by PR 4; T177 by the epic's closing commit; the KT9 box is ticked when PR 4 merges and its Fable review is done.

### 8. Scenarios and controls

Host (`ksys/tests/allocfail.rs`, both modes; tier 0, so they gate every PR): `process_create`; `process_start` of a created process;
`thread_spawn` plain, with a kernel stack region, and SUSPENDED followed by `thread_write_state`; `process_map` then a range unmap and a
protect that split (two splits); kill and teardown of a process with threads and mappings; `port_bind` then a port wait registration and a
commit that passes the baton; `object_wait` and `channel_call` registration plus the commit that wakes them (the harness's `armed_cleanup`
grows to the real epilogue: `WaitTable::commit` with the world's closures); `futex_wait` enqueue and a wake; a whole `sweep` over a live
graph with a garbage cycle. After every run: NO_MEMORY or success, the fuzzer's audits, baseline, and "the same call with memory then
succeeds" (already the harness's rule).

Guest (`init/src/bin/suite.rs` `kernel_allocation_failure_sweep`, `make os-allocfail`, one hart; overnight tier, so only the owner starts
the whole target, but one boot of a new scenario while writing it is an experiment): "spawn a thread and join", "start a process and wait
for its end", "a job tree with a process in a leaf, killed from the root", "object wait woken by another thread", "port wait many",
"channel call answered by a server thread", "futex wait and wake". Each run also checks `debug_stat` 207 (class-N bytes) against a
budget of 64 KiB per call, and the existing VMO, object and ledger baselines. Then one **persistent** pass per scenario (`debug_stat` 208):
calls return NO_MEMORY, nothing panics, the waiter is still woken or killed, baselines return when memory does.

Negative controls and mutation checks (rule 9: each run once by hand when its test is written; none becomes a standing boot):
(1) the existing planted `Vec::push`, moved by a `fault-alloc-plant` feature into `WaitTable::register`: the guest sweep must print
`##PANIC` with "object wait" and N; (2) the class-N predicate with the `TT`/`RQ` clause removed: the persistent pass must panic in a wake

### 10. Risks and what to measure

- **Stack frames** (clippy `large_stack_frames` at 1 KiB; the optimised kernel's 9 KiB budget, checked by the boot's high-water test):
  `FBox::try_new(Process {..})` and `UThread` move a value through a `Vec::push`. Keep the constructors `#[inline(never)]`, print
  `size_of` of `Process`, `UThread` and `Region` once in a host test, and compare the release boot's high-water line before and after
  PR 2 and PR 3. `object_wait`, `port_wait_many` and `channel_call` carry a KT13 allow already: the wait change should shrink them.
- **Instruction counts** (`bench/counts.tsv`, the 5 percent rule): `thread_create_join`, `event_signal_wait`, `handle_dup_close`,
  `page_fault_fresh_page`, `futex_wake_no_waiter`, and the zero-spurious-wake counts of `wake_baseline_bystanders_and_counters`. Small
  maps should get cheaper (no node allocation per insert); if `regions` lookups on the fault path get slower, raise that map's CHUNK.
- **Memory per process and thread**: an `FMap`'s first insert allocates a whole chunk. Measure heap bytes of an idle process and thread
  before and after; MM5's `a_confined_program_cannot_hold_much_uncharged_kernel_memory` is the tripwire.
- **The single registrar** makes a thread visible to `thread_suspend` and kill at spawn, always. EX1's model, the SUSPENDED tests and the
  never-started-child leak tests cover it.
- **The `AsCell` pool** needs the cell unshared at teardown, exactly as `AsCell::take` panics otherwise today (`trap.rs` 198, 753, 1185
  clone it transiently): same invariant, same check.
- **Rehash order** in the wait table is the one subtle step: hence the model's rehash-in-the-middle case and its mutation.
- **The reserve is an estimate, not a proof**: set 256 bytes per thread and 64 KiB per call from `debug_stat` 207's measured peaks (record
  them in `docs/records/`); a fragmented free-list heap can still defeat a large class-N request. That residual is the first item of
  section 7 and shrinks with the slab-over-frames allocator of KERNEL-V1-SPEC 4.2.
- **The sweep's length**: one boot at one hart, 300 s. A process-start scenario costs far more per N than a channel write: measure N and
  seconds per scenario on the first run, and split the heavy scenarios into their own boot rather than raising the timeout.
