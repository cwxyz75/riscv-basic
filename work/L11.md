## L11a built (2026-11-18)

The owner approved the design note's recommendations (Q-L11 closed). L11a is done: `Sched2`'s shared state is in atomics (`pending`, `online`, `pokes`), each thread's home is an atomic in a thread table (`Home`, the spec's `TT`), and placement and push read published per-hart hints (`cur_eff`, `queued_len`, a `would_run` mask) instead of other harts' queues; own-hart and queued-thread reads are unchanged. The kernel is untouched. Checked by `kcore/tests/sched_smp_l11a.rs`, which drives the new scheduler and a frozen copy of the old one (`sched_smp_v0`, delete with L11b) call for call and compares everything observable (two hand mutations caught: hints not marked stale, a pull not updating the home), by every existing model, `make os-test` (loom and shuttle) and the QEMU suite at `-smp 2` and `-smp 4`. Next: L11b (the per-hart locks).

## L11b built (2026-11-18)

Per-hart `RQ` locks and the `TT` table lock (OD125, T156). `Sched2` is generic over `Rqs`; the kernel's `SCHED` lock is gone. Found and fixed on QEMU at `-smp 4`: recording the displaced thread's transit after the lock was released let another hart pull the executing thread (double dispatch); the shuttle model (`kcore/tests/rq_concurrency.rs`) catches that mutation. The lock-order checker now sees lock instances. Left for L11c and L11d: `need_resched`, the dead list and reap by the hart, the idle loop of 3.9, the watchdog, and the negative-control kernels. The frozen pre-refactor copy (`sched_smp_v0`) stays as the differential reference until L11c changes behaviour.

Review of L11b (fresh-context Fable, 2026-11-18): no double-execution or lost-thread path; fixed: a home re-check fooled by a thread that migrated away and back (a table entry now means retry; only a reap makes a thread gone); a poke for the issuing hart itself was dropped by `send_pokes` and left it marked for a tick (the kernel now sends itself an IPI); `balance` re-checks the length gap and priority under both locks; the shuttle model now also drives exit, reap, sleep and set_priority, delivers pokes (own bit included), asserts every live wake succeeds, and checks GP1 and that no mark is left without an IPI at quiescence (the own-poke mutation is caught; the ABA race needs three ordered migrations and is not reachable by the model). INVARIANTS: RQ1 added, K1 and K5 updated.

## L11c part 1 built (2026-11-19)

The dead list in the form the big kernel lock allows: `reschedule` records the thread it switches away from in `PerHart.prev`; `finish_switch` (run by the thread that resumes on that hart, after `Sched2::switched`) frees it if it has exited and is detached (`reap_if_dead`: scheduler record, `KThread`, kernel stack). The reaper thread, its queue and the zombie list are gone: a thread that ends no longer costs a wake and two switches. A joined thread is still freed by its joiner (the big lock keeps the exiting thread's switch complete before any joiner runs; L12 changes that and gives `join` the same hand-over). Checked by `make kquick` (the k boot at four harts: `thousands_of_threads_come_and_go`, `many_threads_do_not_leak`, the detached user-thread tests). Left for L11c part 2: `need_resched` (a wake sets the flag; the switch happens at the defined points), then L11d.

## L11c part 2 built (2026-11-19)

`need_resched` (SCHED-SMP-SPEC X7: a wake never switches). `Sched2` has a deferred mode (`set_deferred`, set by the kernel): a wake, a spawn or a priority change that would make the caller's own hart look at its queues marks the hart (`pending`) instead of settling at once, so `current_on` stays the thread that is running until the hart's defined point calls `reschedule_on`. The kernel (`thread::note_resched`, `preempt_point`) sends the pokes the wake owes and sets `PerHart.need_resched`; the switch happens at the end of the outermost `irq::without`, at the return to user mode (`trap.rs`), or at the thread's own block, yield or exit. First attempt (the switch deferred with the designation changed at wake time) hung one k boot in four: a `block` between the wake and the switch blocked the woken thread, not the running one; the deferred mode is the fix, found by `os-repeat` (10 of 10 after). Checked by: a shuttle model with deferred wakes (`no_thread_executes_on_two_harts_when_wakes_are_deferred`, 20000 schedules, beside the immediate-mode one), the kernel test `a_wake_switches_at_the_end_of_the_outermost_section` (mutation: switching inside `note_resched` is caught), `os-repeat os-ktest-4` 10 of 10, and the k boots at 1, 2 and 4 harts and the release kernel. Left: L11d (the idle loop, the watchdog, the negative controls).

Review of L11c part 2 (fresh-context Fable, 2026-11-19): found `thread::exit` waking its joiners after its own settle, which left `pending` set with no flag and no poke owed (the hart idles until its tick; a hang once L13 removes the tick): fixed (`exit` settles before its switch; `finish_switch` re-raises the flag when a mark is still set). Also fixed: the first user entry and the tick and IPI returns from user mode are defined points. The deferred shuttle model had no `need_resched` flag and no exit-and-wake arm, so it could not see this; it has both now (a mutation that drops the exit settle is caught: "marked but no IPI reached it"). `os-repeat os-ktest-4`: 6 of 6 after the fixes.
