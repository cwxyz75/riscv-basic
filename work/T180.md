title: The direct map reaches 64 GiB of physical space; the spec's target is 128 GiB of RAM, and QEMU puts the device tree at the top of RAM
refs: [PL4, KERNEL-V1-SPEC, SC1]
---

PL4a (memory discovery) maps physical space through the direct map at `DIRECT_BASE`, and stops at `abi::layout::DIRECT_MAP_PHYS_LIMIT` (64 GiB, top-level slots 256 to 319) because slot 320 is the self-test region, 384 the kernel stacks, 416 the heap and 510 the image. RAM starts at 2 GiB on `virt`, so the most RAM it can use is about 62 GiB, against the spec's "up to 128 GiB of RAM" (KERNEL-V1-SPEC section 1). Two consequences, both loud and neither silent: RAM above the limit is left unused (the kernel prints `kernel: RAM above 64 GiB is not used`), and a machine whose firmware puts the device tree above the limit, as QEMU does at the top of RAM (`-m 80G`: the tree sits near 82 GiB), stops at boot with `a device tree below 64 GiB ...`, because the tree cannot be read before the kernel table exists. Why acceptable for now: the development and CI machines have a few GiB, and PL4a's acceptance sizes (128M to 8G) pass. Risk: a larger machine does not boot. Proper fix: move the self-test region (`LAZY_TEST_BASE`), the stack window and the heap window to slots above the direct map's 128 GiB span (they are layout constants with tests: `abi::layout`), raise `DIRECT_MAP_PHYS_LIMIT` to 128 GiB and the early table's loop with it, and for the device tree above the span either map its page through a small temporary window or move the early table's reach with the tree's address (the pointer is known in `a1`); add a boot test at the largest RAM the host can give. SC1 (64 harts) touches the same layout and is the natural place.
