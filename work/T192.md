title: Sweep the kernel, the servers and the libraries for quadratic-time (or worse) algorithms

The phase 5 epic gate failed at four harts because every thread exit woke every sibling and scanned every thread: O(n^2) for n threads, minutes for 2047 of them, and invisible at one hart (the test took under two seconds). It had been there since slice W and passed because the machine was fast enough. Nothing looked for it: the tests that exist check behaviour, not how cost grows. The sweep: (1) read every loop over a collection in `kernel/`, `kcore/`, `ksys/` and the servers (`init/src/bin/*`, `svc`, `pathres`, the net crates) for one that sits inside another loop or inside something called once per element (a scan per syscall, per wake, per exit, per packet, per directory entry, per handle); give each a verdict (bounded by a small constant, by a quota, or unbounded) and list the unbounded ones as items or fix them. Look at the global maps keyed by process or thread id first (`thread_pid`, `starts`, `procs`, `objs`, the wait table, the futex table, the port bindings, the job tree, the VMO store `find_gap`), the free-list and cursor searches, and `Vec::remove(0)`, `contains` on a `Vec`, `retain` and `sort` inside loops. (2) Add a scaling check to the suite: the cost of an operation at n = 64 and at n = 1024 (threads, handles, mappings, bindings, children, directory entries) must grow less than a set factor, using the guest's instruction counter (`os-count` already has it), so a quadratic path fails a gate instead of timing out one. (3) The tests that take more than 5 seconds of guest time (`thread_limit_and_slots_are_reused` and `two_thousand_threads_live` were 50 to 90 seconds at four harts) get progress marks so a stall report shows where. Why acceptable: the known case is fixed (PR 331) and the counts are bounded by the quotas. Risk: another path of this kind fails a gate on a slower runner, or a system with many objects is slow for no visible reason.

Progress (2026-11-21), a first pass by grep for loops over collections in `kernel/`, `ksys/` and `kcore/` (`retain`, `iter().filter`, `contains` on a `Vec`, sums over `procs`): the two unbounded ones found were the per-exit sibling wake and the pid scans of `thread_pid` (fixed: PR 331 and T191). What is left looked bounded by a constant or by a quota: `total_threads` (a sum over the live processes, at most a few dozen, on each thread creation), the run-queue and timer-queue `retain` in `sched2` and `sched` (one queue, at most the 4096 user threads), the wait table's `retain` over the waiters of one object (a mass wake of 2047 threads on one event is quadratic in the waiters but runs in about a second in the thread-limit test), `futex` and `ipc` bindings (bounded by the job's object quota). Not done: the pass over `init/src/bin/*`, `svc`, `pathres` and the net crates; the scaling check in the suite (part 2: instruction counts at n = 64 and n = 1024 live threads, handles, mappings and bindings); the progress marks for the slow tests (part 3). The grep cannot show a cost that grows through a path that looks constant (an operation that walks a list the caller made long), so part 2 is the real test.
