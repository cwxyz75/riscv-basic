### T172: 37 kernel functions have a stack frame over 1 KiB (KT13 allows them, audited)

*(area kernel, horizon near, refs [KT13, KERNEL-V1-SPEC])*


KT13 denies `clippy::large_stack_frames` at 1 KiB (`kernel/clippy.toml`) in the kernel crate. Thirty-seven functions already exceeded it, mostly the syscall layer (`syscall`, `process_start`, `channel_call`, the `process_create` closure: up to about 5 KiB in the debug build's MIR estimate), the self-tests, `kmem::init` and the kernel-half snapshot (`[u64; 256]` arrays). Each carries `#[allow(clippy::large_stack_frames)] // KT13: audited`. Why acceptable: the boot-time high-water check (`kernel_stack_budget`, budget 8 KiB of the 16 KiB stack) measures the real depth of every kernel thread in every boot, and the deepest use so far is well inside it; the lint's estimate counts every local at once, which the compiler's frame layout does not. Risk: a path the suite does not take could be deeper than the check has seen. Proper fix: shrink the large locals (box or reuse the arrays, split the big functions), starting with the syscall dispatch path, and remove each allow as it goes; `grep -rn 'KT13: audited' kernel/src` lists them. With TS3c, which moves most of the syscall frames out of `user.rs`.

### T173: The optimised kernel's deepest stack is the reap on a fresh thread (9.4 KiB); bring it under 8 KiB

*(area kernel, horizon near, refs [KT13, T172])*


KT13's boot-time budget (8 KiB of the 16 KiB kernel stack) found, in the release build, that 1205 of the "user" kernel threads in the suite sit at 9352 bytes of high water, and the rest at 7040: not the syscall paths (the stack depth at syscall entry is only 656 bytes) but what runs before the thread's first line, `thread_bootstrap`: `switch_done`, then `reap_prev` under the big kernel lock, which frees the previous thread's stack and bookkeeping (stack pages and their page tables, the run-queue records, the `BTreeMap` removals) on the fresh thread's stack. Under optimisation the inliner merges those frames. Measured by printing the high water at the entry of `user_thread_main`, `syscall` and `teardown`. Why acceptable for now: optimised builds are held to 9 KiB (`thread::STACK_BUDGET`, cfg `kernel_optimized` from `kernel/build.rs`), the debug build to 8 KiB, and the guard page catches an actual overflow; 12 KiB still leaves a quarter of the stack. Risk: the reap path is the one place where the deepest known chain sits under a trap's frames. Proper fix: do the reap from a shallower place (the idle thread, or split `kstack::free` and the table removals into non-inlined steps with the large locals boxed: `kstack.rs:108` is one of the T172 frames), then drop the optimised budget to 8 KiB and delete the cfg.

**Update (2026-11-24).** `reap_prev`, `reap_if_dead`, `note_stack_use` and the drop of the reaped record are now separate non-inlined frames: the fresh thread's high water in the release build fell from 9352 to 8248 bytes, and the optimised budget is 9 KiB. Still to find: the remaining 56 bytes over 8 KiB (not in the reap chain itself; look at `switch_done` and `thread_bootstrap`), then drop the budget to 8 KiB and delete the cfg.

### T68: Kernel stack slots are never returned

*(area kernel, horizon long, risk L)*


#### Shortcut

**Kernel stack slots are never returned** (slice T step 1): a slot keeps its four frames and its mapping once made and goes on a free list, so memory held is the peak thread count times 16 KiB, and `spawn` panics (`expect`) if no slot or frame can be made, as heap exhaustion did before. A remote hart's stale kernel translation could otherwise point a reused slot at the wrong frame.

#### Why it was acceptable

Bounded by the global thread cap; frames are plentiful (256 MiB).

#### Proper fix, and when

A shootdown on shrink (the infrastructure exists), a fallible `spawn` that returns NO_MEMORY, and per-job charging of stacks (slice T steps 4 and 5).

### T160: Thread creation panics when the kernel is out of frames for a kernel stack

*(area kernel, horizon long, risk H)*


**What:** `new_kthread` in `kernel/src/thread.rs` ends in `expect("memory for a kernel stack")`. A user program that starts threads until the frame pool is empty reaches it.

**Why acceptable for now:** found by the KT3 review (2026-11-18); thread counts are bounded by the job limits, and the suite does not drain frames.

**Risk:** H. A user-controlled path to a kernel panic.

**Proper fix:** `new_kthread` returns an error, `thread_spawn` and the process start path reply `NoMemory`, and a test exhausts frames with a job-less process. Tied to KT8 (the assert and allocation blind spots).

### T202: Kernel stacks and page tables are charged to `Pages`, not to `KernelBytes`

*(area kernel, horizon long, refs [MM5, T1])*


MM5 (KERNEL-V1-SPEC 4.7, the MM5 entry in `docs/records/done.md`) restates kernel stacks and page-table frames in bytes under the new kernel-bytes resource, and charges page tables exactly (`PageTable::map` reporting the frames it allocates) instead of by the estimate `length / 4096 / 512 + 4` kept in `table_charge`. Built so far: the resource, object bodies and thread register state. Stacks (`KSTACK_PAGES` per thread) and tables stay on `Pages`, where the job-limit tests of the suite count them (`thread_stacks_are_charged_to_the_job_and_credited_back`, `page_tables_are_charged_to_the_job`, the 64-page hog test), and the table charge is still the estimate.

Why acceptable: both are charged to the job and limited (J1); only the unit and the exactness differ. Risk: a table frame a fault allocates beyond the estimate is uncharged (bounded by the process limits). Proper fix: with the QEMU acceptance test of MM5, move both to `KernelBytes` in one change with the suite tests that read them, and make the table charge exact.

### T164: The fault-early-reap control proves a missing record panics, not that a reused stack is caught

*(area testing, horizon near, risk L)*


#### Shortcut

`fault-early-reap` (L11d, PR 264) reaps the exiting thread before its switch, record and stack together; the switch then finds no record and the kernel panics, which `teardown_with_threads_on_other_harts` reports as a crash. The Fable review (2026-11-19) pointed out a faithful control frees only the stack and keeps the record: that was tried, and nothing detects it, because a freed `KStack` is neither unmapped nor poisoned, so the thread simply goes on running on memory that is still intact.

#### Why acceptable

The record-removal variant still shows the harness detects a premature reap; the real protection is the big lock (T163) until L12.

#### Proper fix, and when

A debug feature of `KStack`: poison a stack on free (and fill the guard), so a thread still running on a freed stack, or a stack handed out again while in use, fails the guard check ("stack of thread ... was overwritten") the way SCHED-SMP-SPEC 3.6 expects. Then make `fault-early-reap` free only the stack. With L12.

### T82: New kernel pages (heap, stacks) assume a local-only `sfence.vma`

*(area kernel, horizon long, risk L)*


#### Shortcut

**New kernel pages (heap, stacks) assume a local-only `sfence.vma`** (Fable review): growth and stack mapping flush on the mapping hart only; correct while an invalid-to-valid change cannot be cached remotely except by speculative walks, which RVA23 (Svvptc) allows to be absent

#### Why it was acceptable

Svvptc in the target profile

#### Proper fix, and when

Assert Svvptc from the profile check, or broadcast a flush on growth

### Design, per open part

**T164: poison on free, checked at the next hand-out.**
1. In `Drop for KStack`: fill the stack with `POISON` (a constant that is neither 0 nor `CANARY`), guard words included. If the caller's own `sp` is inside this stack (the control, or a real premature reap), fill only the words below `sp` less a margin of 512 bytes, and record the count of poisoned words with the slot (`Slots.free: Vec<(u32, u32)>`).
2. In `kstack::alloc`: before the existing `fill(0)`, check every poisoned word of a recycled slot; a changed word is a panic that names the slot and the word ("kernel stack slot N was written after it was freed"). The zero fill stays (`stack_high_water` relies on it, `kstack.rs:164`).
3. The control (`fault-early-reap`) keeps the record and frees only the stack (`t.stack.take()`), then calls `reschedule()`; its frames land in poisoned words, and the next spawn that pops the slot stops the kernel. The Makefile line changes from `--neg ... teardown_with_threads_on_other_harts` to `--expect-panic "written after it was freed"`.
4. Gate: `cfg(any(debug_assertions, feature = "kstack-poison"))`. Rejected: (a) unmapping a freed stack (the strongest detector, a fault at the first touch): it needs the shrink of T68 and a shootdown per thread exit, which SCHED-SMP-SPEC rejects for user stacks for the same cost; revisit once T68's shrink exists. (b) Poisoning the whole stack while running on it: clobbers the caller's live frames and dies with a wild fault, which is detection without a diagnosis. (c) Checking at switch-out through the guard words: the record of a prematurely reaped thread has no stack (`stack: None`), so `pick_next`'s check (`thread.rs:839`) is skipped.
5. Use volatile reads for the poison check and (T253 item 1, in T256) for `guard_intact` and `stack_used`: same lines, do both.

**T173: the last 56 bytes.** Two routes; take (a) now and leave (b) to T257:
- (a) Measure first, not guess: print `current_stack_use()` at the entry of `switch_done`, `reap_prev` and `user_thread_main` in one release `make kquick` boot, find the frame that carries the 56 bytes (candidates: `thread_bootstrap` inlining `switch_done` with `send_pokes`' poke set, `latency::switch_in`, `program_timer`), mark it `#[inline(never)]` or shrink its local, re-measure, then set `STACK_BUDGET` to 8 KiB in both builds, delete the `kernel_optimized` cfg (`kernel/build.rs:42-45`, `thread.rs:1891-1894`) and fix the stale doc comment.
- (b) The spec's design (SCHED-SMP-SPEC 3.6: `finish_switch` moves the thread to `PerHart.dead`, the hart drains it at a safe point with no lock held, `reap_quiesce()` for leak oracles) removes the reap from the fresh stack altogether. It is T163 inside T257, it changes who pays and when the charge is credited, and it needs a Fable review. Rejected for this item: it is a scheduler-lifetime change riding on a 56-byte problem. If (a) cannot find the bytes in one sitting, stop and do (b) under T257 instead; do not raise the budget.

**T172: shrink the frames in the order the stack sees them.** The budget check bounds the *sum* on paths the suite takes; the allows are per frame. Work by depth, not by count:
1. Syscall dispatch path first (`user/trap.rs`, `user/calls.rs`, `user/waits.rs`, `user/lifecycle.rs`: 32 allows). For each: find the large local (the lint names the size; `[u64; 32]` register images, `ksys::Spawn`, batch arrays of frames, message buffers), and either move the body into an `#[inline(never)]` helper so the big frame is not live across the deeper call, or put the local in an `FBox::try_new` (class R: the call can already reply `NoMemory`). Never box on a class N path.
2. `kmem.rs` (4) and `kstack.rs:110` (the 4-entry frame array is small; the frame is big through inlined `PageTable::map`: make `map` the non-inlined callee) and `thread.rs` (4).
3. Boot-only and test code (`selftest.rs`, `smp_tests.rs`, `sched_tests.rs`, `user_tests.rs`, `dtb.rs`, `entropy.rs`, `main.rs`, `smp.rs`, `kmem::init`: about 30): keep the allow, change the note to `// KT13: boot or test only, on a stack nothing else is under` so the remaining `KT13: audited` count is the real debt. Rejected: boxing boot-time arrays (no benefit, the boot stack is deepest only once and is measured).
4. Add `ksys/clippy.toml` and `kcore/clippy.toml` with the same threshold, or state in the item that they are unlinted; the frames TS3c moved are on the same kernel stack. Rejected: lowering the threshold or raising it to fit: the number is KT13's.

**T68: return slots (the shrink).** Design if the owner wants it now (Q2):
1. `Slots` keeps a count of free slots; when `free.len()` exceeds a high mark (say 64, and more than a quarter of `next`), a trim at a safe point (idle, the same place `heap::service` runs) pops slots, unmaps their four pages under `KPT`, calls `smp::shootdown()` **before** `db.dec` on the frames (TLB2), and puts the slot index on a second list `unmapped` that `alloc` prefers last (it maps four fresh frames into it as `make_slot` does now).
2. `frames_held` becomes mapped slots, not `next`; the level-0 table frames under an emptied 2 MiB stay (64 slots share one; freeing tables is not worth a second shootdown rule).
3. The poison check of T164 does not apply to an unmapped slot (its count of poisoned words is 0).
4. Rejected: unmap on every free (a shootdown IPI per thread exit: `thread_create_join` is a counted benchmark); trimming inside `Drop` (it runs under `irq::off` in `reap_if_dead`'s tail, and a shootdown waits for other harts: latency and a lock-order hazard).

**T202: exact table charge, and the unit.** Independent of Q1, the exactness half can be built:
1. `PageTable::map` and the region mappers in `kcore::aspace` return the number of table frames they allocated (and `unmap`/`destroy` the number freed); `aspace` keeps a per-address-space `table_frames` counter that a host model checks against a walk of the tree.
2. Replace the estimate and the `table_charge`/`charge_end` maps (`ksys/src/proc.rs:125-160`) with charge-as-allocated: a table frame is charged when the walk allocates it (fault path included: this closes the item's "a fault allocates beyond the estimate" hole) and credited when freed; teardown credits the counter. This needs the fault path to refuse cleanly when the charge is refused (AS2's quiet path falls back to the full path on "no memory" already).
3. The unit (`Pages` or `KernelBytes`) is one constant in that code. Do not pick it: Q1.
Rejected: keeping the estimate and adding a fault-time top-up (two bookkeeping schemes for one resource).

**T82 residual (small, in PR B).** In `trap.rs` the `bkl::violates(Class::Vmo)` fatal check (`:760`) runs **before** `stale_entry` (`:769`), and `stale_entry` needs `kmem::get_as()` (class `KSTACK` instance 2, between `JOBS` and `FRAME`). So on hardware that caches invalid entries, a stale entry met while a memory lock is held (first touch of a page `heap::grow` mapped on another hart) is still fatal. Fix: a lock-free read-only walk of the kernel root for the faulting address (kernel table frames are never freed today; with T68's shrink only leaf entries change, tables stay: keep that rule and write it in the SAFETY comment), done before the lock check. Not testable on QEMU (it never caches invalid entries): say so in the PR; a host test of the walk over `kcore::pagetable` is the only check available.

### Invariants and locks

- **C1 / K5 / lock table row 10** (`docs/reference/INVARIANTS.md:42,151,171`): `KSTACK` is class 10 with three instances: 0 the slot allocator (`kstack::SLOTS`), 1 `kmem::KPT`, 2 the kernel address-space object (`kmem::get_as`). `make_slot` takes 0, then 1, then `kmem::frames()` (`FRAME`, class 11). K5: a guard of `TT` instance 0 is never held while a kernel stack is freed (`reap_if_dead` drops `k` first, `thread.rs:764-768`): the poison fill in `Drop` must keep that, and must not allocate.
- **X3, X7**: nothing held across a switch; the T68 trim must run where no lock is held (a shootdown waits for other harts).
- **TLB2 / SMP5** (`:196`, `:98`): a frame that was mapped is freed only after every other online hart has flushed. T68 is the first unmap of *kernel* stack mappings; today's "valid entries of a slot never change" is what makes `kstack.rs`'s module comment true, and `stale_entry`'s walk depends on it too.
- **J1, J2** (`:65,67`): all-or-nothing charge, credited exactly once. T202 moves the credit from "the recorded estimate" to "the frames freed": a table frame freed by `unmap` and again by teardown is a double credit; one allocated on a fault and never counted is a leak. `user::job_ledger` and the `jobs_do_not_leak` scenarios are the oracles.
- **K3** (transit): the faithful control must free the stack in the same window as today (after `exit_on`, before the switch).
- **K6** (`heap::reserve` reads `kstack::LIVE`, `heap.rs:191`): unchanged by poison; unchanged by trim (`LIVE` counts stacks in use, not slots).

**How it goes subtly wrong.**
1. Poison versus the high-water mark: `stack_used` finds the lowest non-zero word; a missed zero fill after the poison check makes every recycled stack read as full and `kernel_stack_budget` fails on every thread (loud, at least).
2. Poison versus the counted benchmark: `count_thread_create_join` (`bench/counts.tsv`, debug kernel, one hart) gains two 2048-word passes per thread; either build the count kernel without the feature or re-record the baseline in the PR and say so.
3. Poisoning from `Drop` when `sp` is in the stack: get the margin wrong and the control dies with a wild fault, which a `--expect-panic` on the message then reports as a failed control, correctly; do not weaken the match.
4. T172 boxing on a class N path turns an infallible path into one that can fail under the allocation-failure sweep (KT9): the host and guest sweeps are the check, and they run in the gates, not locally.
5. T172 `#[inline(never)]` helpers change the release frames again; T173's 56 bytes must be re-measured after T172, not before (hence one PR).
6. T202 on the quiet fault path (AS2): the charge needs `JOBS` while `AS` and `VMO` are held: order AS, VMO, JOBS, FRAME is already the path's order; a refusal must leave the pager-need slot as found.
7. T68: `frames_held` feeds the leak oracle (`kmem.rs:720`, `user_tests.rs:26`); a trim between an oracle's two reads moves frames from "held" to "free" and must leave the sum equal (count both under one lock take).
8. T265's T238 (`thread_stacks_are_charged...` failed once with NO_MEMORY at "the thread now fits") reads the very numbers PR C changes: find its root cause first or the exact check will inherit the flake.

- **Q1 (T202, unit).** WORK-PLAN's MM5 line (2026-11-21) keeps stacks and tables on `Pages`; T202 asks to move them to `KernelBytes`, whose limit is derived as an eighth of the page limit in bytes. Under that fraction a job needs a page limit of 32 to afford one 16 KiB kernel stack and a 40-page job gets 20 KiB for stacks, tables and objects together. Move them (and then: keep the one-eighth fraction, or change it, or give `KernelBytes` a limit of its own in `job_set_limits`, which changes the limits record), or leave the unit and build only the exact table charge?
- **Q2 (T68, shrink).** Is returning kernel-stack frames wanted before the freeze? KERNEL-V1-SPEC's table (line 84) records "slots are never unmapped or returned (T68)" as the known state; the cost is the first shootdown of kernel mappings and a Fable review, the gain is memory after a thread-count peak (16 KiB per thread, bounded by the thread cap).
- **Q3 (T82, profile).** The spurious-fault retry stands in for both fixes T82 named. Is that the accepted end state (then T82 closes with PR B's residual), or should the profile check also refuse a machine without Svvptc (it cannot be exercised on QEMU either way)?
- **Q4 (T172, target).** What count of `KT13: audited` allows closes the part: zero outside boot and test code (proposed), or every one including the self-tests?

## Decisions (2026-11-24, OD152)

(1) T202 changes course from the plan's recommendation: kernel stacks and page tables move to `KernelBytes` (the exact table charge is built as the plan's PR C says, but the resource is `KernelBytes`, not `Pages`). The MM5 line in WORK-PLAN that said they stay on `Pages` is out of date; check every limit and test tuned to the old arrangement (`job_info` figures, the 32-page figure, `thread_stacks_are_charged_to_the_job_and_credited_back`). (2) T82: the retry is the end state, no boot refusal; the question whether Svvptc is mandatory in RVA23S64 stays open and unverified (a community tracker says yes; the ratified list was not seen). (3) T172 closes at zero `KT13: audited` allows outside boot and test code. FZ1 timing is free.
