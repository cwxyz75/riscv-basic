title: A process has no list of its own threads: finding them scans every thread in the system
refs: [AB3]
---

Found while debugging the phase 5 epic gate (2026-11-21). `wake_threads_of(pid)` (`kernel/src/user/wake.rs`) finds the threads of a process by walking `thread_pid`, the global map of every user thread in every process, and filtering by pid: O(all threads), not O(this process's threads). The process record has no complete list of its own threads: `Process::thread_objs` holds only threads that have a handle and an object, so a detached thread (`thread_spawn` with DETACHED) is in none. The caller was also quadratic (it ran on every thread exit; fixed in PR 331, which restricts it to whole-process exits), but the lookup is the wrong shape even for a kill. Proper fix: a per-process set of live tids (`BTreeSet<Tid>` or the like) kept in step at spawn, start and exit under the same lock, and used by `wake_threads_of`, the kill and suspend paths and `threads_alive` (which becomes its length, so the two cannot disagree); then look for other places that scan a global map by pid (`thread_pid`, `starts`, `stacks`, `interrupts`). A host test in `ksys` that a process's tid set equals the threads the scheduler has for it after random spawns, exits, kills and detaches. Why acceptable: after PR 331 the scan runs once per whole-process exit or kill, and the thread counts are bounded (`MAX_USER_THREADS` 4096). Risk: a kill of a small process while thousands of threads exist elsewhere costs a walk of all of them under the big lock.
