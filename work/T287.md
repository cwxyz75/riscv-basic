Part of the stability epic (`STAB0`). Gathered on 2026-11-27: T167 and T49 from T267, T222 and T226 from T265 (their text below,
as it was). Each is a thread that ran later than a test allowed, or a wake that may have been lost; the test cannot say which.

**New sighting (2026-11-27, on `main`).** Push of the iommu-drivers merge (`99eaac64`), run 45985524066, job `kernel (ktest 4
harts)`: `harts_go_offline_and_come_back FAIL round 1: the sleepers made 3 wakes in 10 ms with hart 2 offline (baseline 89);
window 10288 us; per hart (ticks, ipis, idle entries, idle us, lock takes; running, state): 0: (1, 1, 0, 0, 1; 5, 2) 1: (0, 2, 0,
0, 2; 148, 2) 2: (0, 0, 0, 0, 0; 3, 0) 3: (1, 0, 0, 0, 2; 4, 2)`. Unlike the 2026-11-26 capture (harts idle 7 to 9 ms of the
window), **no hart entered idle** and the online harts took 0 or 1 tick in 10 ms: three busy harts that neither ticked nor woke
the sleepers. Either the host ran none of them (a pause: T284's detector says), or they ran with interrupts off or never
programmed the sleepers' deadlines. This is the third occurrence of the T49 shape.

## The parts, as they were

### T167: A freshly started user thread was seen taking 0.7 to 2.4 s to start running on an idle two-hart machine

*(area sched, horizon near, risk M)*


#### Shortcut

While writing the L12c test, at `-smp 2` on a loaded development machine, a user thread started with `user::start` was not observed running on any hart for 673, 1474 and 2375 timer ticks (several runs; it passed at once on a quiet host and at four harts), with and without the L12c fast path. Possibly host load stretching a TCG vCPU, possibly a real delay (a runnable thread behind equal-priority work on hart 0 with hart 1 slow to pull). The watchdog (500 ms, two samples) did not fire. Not diagnosed; the test now waits 5000 ticks.

#### Why acceptable

Seen only under heavy host load, never on CI; no wrong behaviour, only latency.

#### Proper fix, and when

Reproduce with `os-repeat` of a boot that starts a user thread at `-smp 2` under CPU load (the T157 burner method), record the thread's queue, hart state and pending marks at 300 ticks, and find where it waits. Before L13 (no tick), where an unmarked idle hart would make it permanent.


### T49: Timing-sensitive tests that flake under host load

*(area testing, horizon long, risk L)*


#### Shortcut

**Timing-sensitive tests that flake under host load.** (Seen once in CI at four harts: `a_thread_killed_before_it_ran_runs_no_user_code` got BAD_STATE on its kill because the thread had already run and ended the child; it passed on rerun. The test assumes a freshly created thread has not run yet, which SMP does not promise.) (Also fixed: `threads_created_but_not_run_when_the_process_ends` raced on SMP, its second thread could end before the first thread left, making the first the last; the second now waits 20 ticks.) `bkl_is_released_on_user_entry_and_idle` (its threshold "lock found held < 1/4 of takes" failed twice at `-smp 4`, with 22% and 47% held, while the host was busy; relaxed to "free at least as often as held", which still fails for a lock held across user mode) and the sleeper-arrival naps in the S7b futex tests (`nap_ticks(15)` to let a thread reach the kernel's table) assume the host keeps up. (`futex_wake` used to wake every blocked thread in the system when it woke a sleeper; since slice W step 2 it wakes exactly the sleepers it removed, by id.)

#### Why it was acceptable

The gate passes on an unloaded machine; a rerun settles a flake.

#### Proper fix, and when

Observe instead of waiting (a readiness signal or a kernel-side sleeper count a test can read); a per-futex wake instead of the global queue wake (T12).

**Seen 2026-11-26: `harts_go_offline_and_come_back` (`kernel/src/smp_tests.rs`), smoke b, four harts** (the epic smoke of `epic/iommu-kernel`, run 45906519497, head 84421c9): "round 6: the sleepers made 11 wakes in 10 ms with hart 2 offline (baseline 78)"; the bound is a quarter of the baseline. Not reproduced under rule 11: five `make os-boot-test-smoke-b` runs of that head (four harts) and 35 kernel-only boots at four harts all passed on a quiet host. Under heavy host overload (other QEMU boots in parallel) the same head failed 5 of 203 and the fork point (58894ca, before the IOMMU changes to `hart_set_online`) 1 of 203, so it is load-sensitive on both. A diagnostic build (per-hart tick, IPI, idle-entry, idle-time and big-lock deltas over the window, printed on failure) caught one: elapsed 10.1 ms of guest time, harts 0 to 2 idle 7 to 9 ms of it with 4 to 6 ticks and about 10 lock takes each, 20 wakes against a baseline of 88. The sleepers (0.3 ms sleeps) were not woken on time by idle harts, which is what a stalled vCPU thread looks like (guest time runs on while the host does not schedule the hart) and also what a timer left on the wrong hart by the evacuation would look like for one window. The failure message now carries those per-hart deltas (the iommu-kernel smoke-fix PR). Next occurrence: look at whether the sleepers' deadlines sit on an online hart's timer; a stalled vCPU shows every hart idle with few ticks, a misplaced timer shows the deadlines on the offline hart.


### T222: once_and_semaphore failed once in the release ktest (two posts, two taken: got 1)

*(area testing, horizon long, risk L, refs [T167, T178])*


#### Shortcut

`once_and_semaphore` failed once in `kernel (ktest release)` on the epic PR of phase 6d (PR 421, run 45054605311): "two posts, two taken: got 1, want 2", and with it `init_runs_in_user_mode_and_passes`. The re-run of the job passed. The test starts three semaphore sleepers, naps 30 ticks, posts twice and naps 60 ticks, then counts the takers; the semaphore (`rt::sync::Semaphore`) is correct for any order of start and post, so either a sleeper did not run within 90 ticks on a loaded runner (T167: a fresh user thread was seen taking 0.7 to 2.4 s to start) or a futex wake was lost (L14's lock-free `futex_wake`).

#### Why it is open

TESTING.md rule 11: an intermittent kernel-test failure is a defect with a work item, not a re-run.

#### Proper fix, and when

Reproduce under the perturbation run (KT7) and in a loop at `-smp 4` release. If a thread was only late, the test waits for the takers with a deadline (poll `SEM_TAKEN` up to the 5 s the workers allow) instead of a fixed nap, and T167 carries the start latency; if a wake was lost, that is a futex defect and the fix is in `kcore::futex` / `futex_wake`'s quiet path. Do this with the next change to the futex path (phase 6e).


### T226: a_thread_killed_before_it_ran_runs_no_user_code is a race at more than one hart

*(area testing, horizon long, refs [SC2, L20])*


The test makes a thread in a child (`thread_on_stack`, free placement) that would exit(42) at once, and kills the child straight after. On one hart the thread cannot start before the parent's next syscall; on four it can start on another hart as soon as it exists, and it won the race in the first boot of phase 6f (the parent's syscall return became lock-free and the child's start no longer waits for the big lock, which moved the odds). The test now tries up to forty times and requires three runs (or at least one in forty) where the kill came first, and no outcome other than the kill's code or the thread's own. A deterministic version needs the placement masks of SC2 (pin the new thread to the parent's hart, which does not yield until the kill), then this item closes.

## Stability epic (2026-11-27)

**Causes to name.** T49 (`harts_go_offline_and_come_back`): host pause, or the sleepers' timers left on the offline hart by the
evacuation (PM1), or a hart running with interrupts off; the three are told apart by T284's pause detector and by where the
sleepers' deadlines sit at the failure. T167 (a fresh user thread not running for 0.7 to 2.4 s at two harts; the ~100 ms stall
of T178's 2026-11-26 note): where it waits (queue, hart state, pending marks at 300 ticks). T222 (`once_and_semaphore`: got 1):
late sleeper or lost futex wake. T226: not an intermittent at all but a test race; OD160 decided a test-only pin.

**Scope (one PR, plus the fix the evidence names).** T222's redesign from T265's plan (section 2 item 5: workers record how they
got the permit, the test polls with a 10 s deadline and fails "lost wake" on a permit taken after a timeout: mind the trap that
a poll at or above the workers' deadline hides a lost wake); T226's test-only pin (OD160: a request that sets the affinity of
the next thread the suite creates, test kernels only, documented as such in `SYSCALLS.md`); T49's failure message extended with
each sleeper's deadline and the hart whose timer holds it, and the pause record from T284; T167's state dump at 300 ticks.
If a kernel cause is shown, its fix is a second, small PR of this item with its own regression test.

**How to investigate.**
- T49 first, it is the one seen on `main`: run the four-hart kernel-only boot with T284's injector (`OS_HOST_STALL`) and see
  whether the failure's signature (idle entries 0, ticks 0 or 1) appears; then without the injector under load, and under
  `OS_RECORD` (one TCG thread: no host pause of a single hart is possible, so a failure there is the kernel's). Read
  `hart_set_online`'s evacuation of timers (`kernel/src/thread.rs`, `kcore::sched_smp`) for a deadline that stays on the hart
  going offline.
- T222: write the host model first: `rt::sync::Semaphore` over a futex is small enough for **loom** (or shuttle) on the host
  with a model futex (wake, wait with timeout); that is an independent oracle for "no lost wake" in the user-side protocol.
  The kernel side has `kcore/tests/futex_model.rs` and `futex_concurrency.rs`. For the whole wait/wake path across the user
  library and the kernel, a **TLA+** spec (needs an OD, `STAB0`) is the right tool if the two models pass and the failure
  recurs. The owner's chaos run for T222 (OD160) is one of the owner runs of `STAB0`.
- T167: the T157 burner method (CPU load on the host) with `make kquick`-style boots at two harts, bounded to an hour; dump the
  run queue and marks of every hart when the started thread has not run at 300 ticks.
- Repeat to count, bounded: `make os-repeat TARGET=os-ktest-4 N=15` (about an hour) per variant.

**Acceptance (each run under two hours).** The semaphore host model (loom/shuttle) with a mutation (drop one wake in the model's
`post`) that it catches; the suite's `once_and_semaphore` with the wake record, mutation-checked once by hand (a dropped wake in
a local `rt::sync::Semaphore::post` makes it say "lost wake"); T226 deterministic at every hart count with the pin (the
forty-try loop goes); each of T49, T167, T222 struck on a cause shown (OD160) or left open with the record of the bounded hunt.
Review by Opus.

## 2026-11-28: the bounded hunt and what it showed (the T287 PR)

All runs on the development Mac (10 cores, shared with other tasks), the kernel-only shard (`OS_SHARD=k`), one boot at a time unless
said; each boot 1.5 to 2.5 minutes. Nothing here is a soak, a smoke or an owner run.

**T222 (`once_and_semaphore`: two posts, got 1).** The user half is now proved on the host: `rt::sync::Semaphore` runs
`kcore::usync` (the same code), and `kcore/tests/usync_concurrency.rs` checks it under loom (bounded to three preemptions where three
threads share a condition variable) and shuttle (random and PCT, 20,000 iterations each) over a model futex built on the kernel's own
table (`kcore::futex::Futexes`: compare and enqueue under one lock, a wake dequeues, a timeout `forget`s a sleeper still queued). It
passes. Mutations, each caught: the wake dropped from `post` (loom: deadlock in two models; shuttle: three of four fail); a wake only
when the count was 0 (loom deadlock; shuttle two of four). Dropping the taker's last look after a timeout is not caught and is an
equivalent mutant for conservation (a timed-out sleeper consumed no wake, so the post's wake went to a sleeper that takes the permit).
The suite's test now polls (10 s) instead of napping 30 + 60 ticks, the workers (20 s deadlines) record how they took the permit
(`Took`), and a failure says "a lost wake" (a permit left with a worker asleep, or one taken only after a futex timeout well before the
deadline) or "a late thread" (a worker never began to wait). Mutation by hand: the wake dropped from `kcore::usync::post` makes the
four-hart boot fail with "2 permits taken: got 0 in 10000 ticks after the posts; 2 left; a lost wake: a permit is there and a worker
still sleeps". The owner's chaos boots (a9afc56b, 14 harts on a 4-core runner) also failed this test ("got 0" at seed 4, two harts);
the same seed at two harts with the harts under the host's cores passed (below). **Cause of the 2026-11-22 failure: not shown.** The
user protocol is ruled out by the models; the kernel's futex half has its own models (`futex_model.rs`, `futex_concurrency.rs`); the
fixed 90-tick nap was shorter than a fresh thread's start latency on a loaded runner (T167's 0.7 to 2.4 s), which explains "got 1"
without a lost wake but is a reading, not a demonstration. The test now names which it is on its next occurrence. Part stays open.

**T226.** Done as OD160 decided: `debug_stat` 219 (test kernels and the release-suite kernel) pins the next user thread the caller
creates to the hart it is created on; the test spawns the child at a priority below the suite's, runs 20 ms without blocking, then
kills: ten rounds, each must end with the kill's code. The forty-try loop is gone. Mutation by hand: without the pin (219 a no-op)
the four-hart boot failed ("kill before it ran: got BadState": the free thread had started on another hart and ended the child);
with the pin it passed at one and four harts. (With a 2 ms spin the unpinned thread did not start elsewhere in time, so 20 ms.) The
residual risk: the creator itself is free, so a higher-priority thread arriving on its hart during the spin could preempt it and let
another hart pull it, and then the pinned child runs; that is a false failure, never a false pass.

**T49 (`harts_go_offline_and_come_back`, idle entries 0, ticks 0 or 1).** Not reproduced: four-hart boots with `OS_HOST_STALL=10:40`
(seeds 1 to 4, base and branch) and three boots at once (twelve harts on ten cores) passed it. `OS_RECORD` is not available as the
discriminator at more than one hart (this QEMU refuses record and replay with several CPUs), so record mode cannot rule out a pause
here. What was found by reading: the pause detector could not see a hart that was **still** stopped when the window was judged, since
a hart records its lateness only when it takes the late interrupt; the T49 shape (online harts with no idle entry, no idle time and at
most one tick in the window) is what harts frozen through the window and not yet resumed look like. The detector now counts a hart
that is late now (its armed interrupt overdue and not taken: `kcore::pause::covering_with`, `pause::overdue_now`, host-tested), so such
a window is taken again; and the failure message now carries each sleeper's home hart, state and deadline, every hart's next event and
armed timer, the harts late now and the pause record of the window. The evacuation (`kcore::sched_smp::evacuate`) moves a blocked
sleeper with its timer and marks the destination, which pokes it to re-arm; no path that leaves a deadline on the offline hart was
found, and `sched_offline` with its negative control covers it on the host. Next occurrence: a stopped vCPU shows deadlines overdue on
online harts ("harts late now" non-zero on two), a timer left behind shows a sleeper's deadline homed on the offline hart. Part open.

**T167.** `no_big_lock_for` prints `##SLOW` at 300 ticks when the started thread has not run (its state, home and deadline, every
hart's running thread, next event and armed timer, the harts late now, the pause record, and in a debug build the scheduler's queues);
the harness prints it as a note on a passing boot. Not seen in these runs. Part open.

**Leads from the epic's runs.**
- `the_latency_test_can_fail` under `OS_HOST_STALL=10:40`: **cause named**: a negative control is not a timing bound, and the
  three-covered-windows rule made it give up whenever the host was busy (base: failed 2 of 2 such boots, "host pause: three windows
  in a row were covered"). The control (and its user twin) now skips covered windows for at most 200 and stops at the first kept
  window past its bound; branch: passed 4 of 4 such boots (two debug, two release).
- `two_waiters_on_one_queue_wake_in_order` ("got 1, want 2") and `wake_many_makes_every_waiter_runnable_before_any_runs` ("the two
  waiters did not block"): fixed naps of 2 to 5 ticks. A pause of the host inside the nap leaves the napping thread's timer overdue,
  so on resume it preempts the threads it waits for before they run (main is the higher priority on the same hart): the tests now
  poll for what they wait for (`settles`). Not reproduced on purpose; the reading is the mechanism, not a demonstration.
- Short waits with bare deadlines ("a thread ended", 500 ms; "an exception message arrives", 1 s) now use `wait_signal`, the
  pause-aware wait of T265.
- `channel_call_times_out_but_the_request_was_sent` under the injector: not reproduced (two release and two debug boots at 10:40).
  Its bounds already carry 250 ms of slack; in the chaos logs the channel tests fail as a cascade (a reply left queued by an earlier
  failing test is read by the next), not one by one.
- The owner's chaos pieces (two harts, 14 harts in flight on 4 cores): seeds 1, 4 and 10 at two harts, one boot at a time on this
  machine (harts under the free cores), all passed: none of the two-hart failures (`kill_a_spinner_on_another_hart`,
  `user_mode_ticks_take_no_big_lock`, `wait_is_woken_by_a_message`, `once_and_semaphore`, `wake_many...`, `channel_call_*`, the
  leak checks) reproduced, so they are the overload of that runner, not chaos-exposed bugs (one boot per seed: a demonstration that
  they are not deterministic in the seed, not a proof that none is a rare race).
