---
id: T275
kind: tidy
title: Every iodomain_map and iodomain_unmap pays its own invalidation and fence: no batch call
area: drivers
horizon: long
risk: L
complexity: M
change_risk: H
refs: [IOMMU-SPEC, OD166, T42, IOMMU6]
---

**What.** A map invalidates and fences before it returns, and so does every unmap (IOMMU-SPEC 4.3, 6.1; OD166 point 7). A
driver that maps or unmaps many buffers in a row (a network driver with per-packet mappings, a start-up that maps a dozen
pools) pays one command-queue round trip and one wait per call; the spec forbids batching across calls.

**Why acceptable.** The drivers of this system map their buffers once at start and keep them, so the cost is paid a handful of
times per driver life. One fence per call is what makes "no frame is freed before its fence" (IO3) simple to state and to
check.

**Risk.** Performance only, and only for a driver that maps per request.

**Proper fix, and when.** A call that takes several ranges (`iodomain_map_many`, `iodomain_unmap_many`, or a vector form of the
two calls) and runs **one** invalidation sequence and **one** fence for all of them: the leaves of every range are written or
cleared, one fence is queued, and nothing of any range is released before it completes. It keeps IO3 and IO8 as they are (the
fence still precedes every free; a timeout leaks the whole batch). It is new ABI and a spec amendment (the owner's permission),
with the model of IOMMU3 extended to batches. Do it when a driver that maps per request exists. Not before the epic (T42) is
closed.
