Part of the stability epic (`STAB0`). Gathered on 2026-11-27: T209 (from T265), T44 (from T264), and T122 and T269 (merged
here and closed). Their text is below as it was. All four are the storage stack meeting a restart or a slow device: the
volume manager's restart and publish, `fsd`'s start, and the block driver's timeout and restart.

**Since then.** The block path moved to `virtio-blk-pci` behind the IOMMU (IOMMU10) and virtio-mmio left the allowlist (IOMMU12,
2026-11-27): T269's cascade ("map the window: InvalidArgs" after a dead driver) was on the virtio-mmio window, which is gone.
Its first failure (`blk_round_trip`: -12, TIMED_OUT) and the question behind the cascade (does a dead driver leave something
that the next driver cannot map?) apply to the PCI driver's BARs and DMA domain as well. T122's fix (`volmgr` publishes after
`fsd` reports ready) is in the code; the job it flaked in (`harness (fail-demo)`) runs only when the harness changes.

## The parts, as they were

### ~~T209: volmgr_serves_volumes_by_the_table failed once in the phase 8 epic gate (OD140: a defect until its cause is known)~~ (done, 2026-11-27: a demonstrated test race, below)

*(area kernel, horizon long, refs [OD140])*


**What.** In the epic gate of PR 383 (smoke a, 2026-11-21) `volmgr_serves_volumes_by_the_table` failed with "unexpected status -100" at the end of `open_volume` (the 15 s retry of a view open after the volume manager restart), and passed on the next run of the same code plus two small fixes. No cause is known.

**Why it is not just re-run.** TESTING.md rule 11: an intermittent kernel-test failure is a defect until its cause is known. It may be a real race in the restart path, or an effect of the EX1/EX2 changes (the thread park, the exception channel reaping on handle close).

**Proper fix.** Reproduce it from the smoke-a log (the harness prints the volmgr lines around it), find whether the restarted volmgr never published or the open retried against a dead channel, and fix that. Do it when the epic's smoke shows it again or in the first slack between items; it does not block phase 9.

**Second occurrence (2026-11-22).** The same test failed the same way (`unexpected status -100 at suite.rs:11217`) in a full local boot of the latency-trace kernel at four harts with `bench=full` (`make os-latency`, the whole suite, 264 user tests passed). It is a different binary and a far heavier run than the gate's smoke a, so the cause is not the gate's load alone; the `open_volume` retry of 15 s ran out both times. That boot's log has the volmgr lines around it.

### ~~T269: Soak at four harts on a hosted runner: blk_round_trip failed and seven device tests followed it (one occurrence)~~ (done, 2026-11-27, below)

*(area testing, horizon near, refs [T40, T44, T252])*

**What.** Soak run 45526124119 (2026-11-25, `main` at d518353), job `smp`, `OS_SMP=4 os_boot_test.py` on the normal kernel: `blk_round_trip` failed ("the reply carries the boot image: got (-12, 0), want (0, 1)"), then `blk_driver_restart`, `devmgr_starts_blkd_for_the_disk`, `interrupt_object_fires_and_masks`, `fsd_keeps_files_across_a_restart`, `volmgr_serves_volumes_by_the_table`, `symlinks_are_followed_by_the_client_not_the_server` and `init_runs_in_user_mode_and_passes` failed with "map the window: InvalidArgs" (AIA interrupts taken 0). The two-hart boot before it passed. Not reproduced: a local four-hart boot of the same kernel passed (391 tests), and the CI re-run's four-hart boots passed. The earlier soak of 2026-11-23 (run 45199049626) also failed in the `smp` job.

**Why acceptable.** Nothing else points at a kernel fault: T40 records that at four harts a boot sees stalls of 15 to 90 ms on hart 0 which timed out `blk_round_trip` before, and the seven later failures look like a cascade from the first (the driver was left in a state where its window could not be mapped again). Unproven.

**Proper fix.** On the next recurrence keep the failing-boot log (the job uploads it) and read what `blk_round_trip` saw first (-12 is the status to find in the driver) and whether the later `InvalidArgs` is a leaked window from the dead driver. If it never recurs in three soaks, close it as load noise.

## Stability epic (2026-11-27)

**Causes to name.** T209: was the volume never published within 15 s because the budgets do not nest (5 s per Busy device plus
15 s for `fsd` to mount, against the test's 15 s), or because a probe that failed once while the old driver wound down
ignores the device for good (`volmgr.rs`, T265's plan, row T209)? T269: what timed out first, and does a killed `blkd` leave a
BAR mapping, a domain or an interrupt the next one is refused? T122: does it recur at all on the current code (`fsd: exit N`
is printed now)?

**Scope (one PR).** The T209 plan's (a), (b), (c) (T265's plan, section 2 item 4: a bounded probe retry when the device was
Busy a moment before; the test's wait covering the server's own budgets; each `volmgr` stage logged with the clock), T44's
read-only and stalled devices in the harness, and the fix of whatever the evidence names.

**How to investigate.**
- Read the logs first. T209's first occurrence is PR 383's epic gate (smoke a, 2026-11-21): find the run (`gh run list
  --workflow ci.yml`) and read its `volmgr:` lines; T269's is soak run 45526124119, job `smp` (`gh run view --log`): read what
  `blk_round_trip` saw before the -12 and the driver's lines.
- Make the slow device deterministic instead of waiting for a loaded runner: QEMU's block throttling
  (`throttling.iops-total=`, `throttling.bps-total=` on the drive) and `blkdebug` error injection (`-drive driver=blkdebug,...`
  with a rules file: delay or fail the n-th request) give a disk that is slow or fails on cue. T44's read-only device is
  `readonly=on`. These are QEMU features (no OD); check `check-qemu.sh` still accepts the command line.
- Then the restart: a suite test that kills `blkd` mid-request with the throttled disk and starts the next one, and the volume
  manager test with the first probe failing on cue.
- Repeat to count, bounded: `make os-repeat TARGET=os-ktest-4 N=15` (the kernel-only shard has the PCI disks and `blkd`; about
  an hour) with and without throttling. Rule 11: five failed attempts at the same target before calling a sighting
  intermittent; keep the logs in `target/repeat/`.

**Acceptance (each run under two hours).** A host test of `volmgr`'s retry decision behind a pure function (Busy-then-probe-fails
is retried within its bound; a non-volume is not); the throttled-disk and failing-probe suite tests fail on today's code (or
show the budget overrun) and pass after; T44's read-only and stalled-device tests exist and pass (ReadOnly, Timeout, IoError
statuses each asserted); each part is struck on its named cause (OD160) or, if the cause is not shown, left open with the
record of what was run. Review by Opus.

## What was found (2026-11-27, feature PR into `epic/stability`)

Every run below was a local boot on the shared development machine (QEMU 11.1.3), each well under two hours. "Branch" is this PR,
"base" the same tree with the old file put back (scratch). The logs read are CI's: PR 383's smoke a (run 44961318013, job
137020563032) for T209, and soak run 45526124119, attempt 1, job `smp` (artifact `failing-logs-3`, `kernel-1795571117.log`) for T269.

### T209: the test's wait did not cover the server's own budgets (a demonstrated test race)

- **The log.** The second rig said `device 0: not a doofs volume, ignored` and `device 1: doofs volume ...`, and nothing more
  before the test gave up at 15 s. So the probe found the volume (the "ignored for good" hypothesis is ruled out for this
  occurrence), and `volmgr` was in `start`, waiting for `fsd` to mount and say ready, which it allows 15 s. The test's whole
  wait was 15 s, so it ran out while the server was inside its own budget.
- **The reproduction.** The second disk throttled (`OS_DISK_THROTTLE=pd1:bps-total=5600`, a slow device on cue), only
  `volmgr_serves_volumes_by_the_table`, four harts. Base failed with the same message (`unexpected status -100`) and the same
  two lines and nothing after them (32 s boot). The branch published at 16.6 s (probe 5.6 s, mount 11.0 s) inside its new
  bound (81 s boot). Its test then hit rt's 30 s per-request bound on the first file create: at 1.4 blocks/s, `fsd`'s
  copy-on-write create takes longer than that. That is a disk too slow for the client protocol, not the race. At
  `iops-total=2` both base and branch pass (107 s each): the mount fits in 15 s there.
- **Not shown.** What made `fsd`'s mount slow in the two sightings (a CI runner, and the latency kernel at `bench=full`).
- **The fix.** `open_volume` waits `volume_publish_bound()`: for each disk, twice `mounts::discovery::DEVICE_BOUND_MS`, plus
  `READY_MS` (55 s with two disks). It costs time only on a failure. A failure now says what the registry lists.
- **The other suspect was a real defect too, fixed.** A probe that could not read just after a `Busy` made `volmgr` ignore the
  device for good. The new suite test `volmgr_retries_a_busy_device_and_publishes_only_mounted_volumes` shows it: the test
  stands in for the device manager and answers the volume disk's opens `Busy`, then with a dead driver, then for real.
  - Base: `device 1: not a doofs volume, ignored`; the volume was never published; fail at 55 s.
  - Branch: published; the junk disk was opened once.
  - The decision is `mounts::discovery`, a pure state machine. `fsfmt::probe_volume` tells `Unreadable` from `NotVolume`.
- **The decision's proofs.**
  - Host test `mounts/tests/discovery.rs`.
  - Kani, `cargo kani -p mounts`, by hand, about 4 s: every run of up to eight answers keeps the bounds. A volume is used. A
    readable non-volume is never probed again. An unreadable device is retried only after a `Busy`. Nothing is asked at or past
    `DEVICE_BOUND_MS`.
  - Mutation checks. Dropping the `Busy` condition fails the host test and the proof. Dropping the cap fails both.
    `Unreadable` mapped to `NotVolume` fails `fsfmt`'s new test.
- **The diagnostics.** Each `volmgr` stage is logged with the clock and its length (`volmgr: t=<ms> device N: ... (probe k took
  N ms, M ms in all, after Busy)`, `volume ...: ready in N ms, published`, `did not come up (it ended | no word after N ms)`).
  The next slow start names its stage.

### T122: a name published for a server that then exits (the mechanism shown, the fix kept)

- **The test.** The second half of the new test writes a newest superblock with another geometry: the probe skips it and finds
  the volume, and `fsd`'s mount takes it and fails (`fsd: exit 5: mount: SizeMismatch`). Once `volmgr` has looked at every
  device and two more seconds have passed, the name must be absent (`NOT_FOUND`). It is.
- **The mutation.** Publishing at spawn, as before T122's fix, makes the same test fail with `Err(-109)`: the name was published
  and went stale with the dead server, which is T122's symptom class.
- **Not known.** Why `fsd` exited in the fail-demo runs of 2026-11-17 (no log was kept).
- **The record.** In the 56 `harness (fail-demo)` runs from 2026-11-22 to 2026-11-27 there were two failures, both
  `teardown_with_threads_on_other_harts` (T265's area), and no volume test. This is noted, not used as evidence.

### T269: the first failure was the suite's own 500 ms bound; the cascade was the test rig's error path (named from the log and the code)

- **The first failure.** At `d518353` the first `boot_image_vmo()` of that boot ran inside `blk_rig`.
  `fetch_boot_image_vmo`'s `channel_call` had a 500 ms deadline, and its -12 is `TIMED_OUT` (the test took 599 ms).
- **The cascade.** The old `blk_rig` mapped the virtio-mmio window (`find_blk`) before that fetch. The fetch's `?` returned
  before the `BlkRig` (whose `Drop` unmaps) existed, so the window stayed mapped in the suite. Every later `find_blk` got
  `process_map` InvalidArgs at `MMIO_VA`: "map the window: InvalidArgs", seven tests. Nothing was left by a dead driver.
- **The same class on the PCI path, found with the throttled disk and fixed.** `fsd_keeps_files_across_a_restart` returned on a
  failure without ending its device manager, driver and servers. With `OS_DISK_THROTTLE=pd1:iops-total=4` it failed, and every
  later test of the disk failed with `## devmgr: pci 00:06.0 claim failed Domain` (the old claim's domain lived on).
  - The test, and `devmgr_starts_blkd_for_the_disk` and `volmgr_serves_volumes_by_the_table`'s setup, now hold `KillOnDrop`
    (and `KillProcsOnDrop` for the servers).
  - Same throttle, after the fix: the later tests pass (398 s boot, against 148 s, branch and base).
- **The fetch.** Its bound is 10 s, `blk_rig` fetches before it holds anything, and every fetch prints its time (`suite: the boot
  image (8628 KiB) came in 18 ms` at four harts here).
- **Not shown.** What held that one reply past 500 ms on the runner. The soak predates the pause detector; the next one prints
  both the fetch time and any `##PAUSE`.
- **The question on the PCI path.** Does a dead driver leave something that refuses the next one? No. The new Kernel test
  `blk_reports_read_only_io_errors_and_timeouts` shows it on the throttled fault disk:
  - A driver whose device holds its read past two seconds answers `Timeout` and exits 10.
  - A driver killed while the device still holds its read is followed by one whose bring-up works (BAR, new domain, vectors).
  - Domains, their pages and vectors return to their baseline.

### T44: the read-only, failing and stalled devices exist

- **The fault disks.** The kernel-only boot carries two (`PCI_FAULT_DISKS` in the harness; `TEST-TOOLS.md`, "A slow or failing
  disk on cue").
  - `00:08.0` is read-only (`readonly=on`), with a `blkdebug` EIO on sector 64.
  - `00:09.0` is throttled to 2 KiB/s, so the second of two back-to-back requests waits about four seconds.
- **The test.** `blk_reports_read_only_io_errors_and_timeouts` asserts `Info` read-only, `ReadOnly` for a write and a flush,
  `IoError` for the failing read (and the driver serves on), and `Timeout` with exit 10. It kills a driver while the device
  certainly holds its request.
- **Mutation checks, one boot each.**
  - `read_only: false` in `blkd`: fails at "a read-only disk".
  - `TIMEOUT` 10 s: fails, "no read of the throttled disk timed out".
  - An I/O error mapped to `Ok`: fails at "a read the device fails".
- **The harness** fails a kernel-only boot that does not print the test's end line. `check-qemu.sh` checks CI's QEMU has
  `blkdebug`.
- **Closed by tests that already exist.** A client that never reads replies: `blkd` serves through `rt::service::Core`, whose
  hold-and-expire logic is host-tested (`svc/tests/model.rs`) and exercised in QEMU by memfsd's slow-client test.
- **Remains unverifiable.** Flush: QEMU's raw writes land in the host page cache either way.

### Also found: `fsd_keeps_files_across_a_restart` assumed the kill beat `fsd`'s 2 s commit timer

- **The failure.** Under the slow disk the test failed with "the unsynced file is gone". The steps between the first unsynced
  change and the kill outlasted `COMMIT_INTERVAL`, and the timer committed a prefix of the changes. A host pause of two seconds
  would do the same.
- **The fix.** The test now measures the window.
  - Within the interval: strictly the synced state.
  - Past it: any in-order prefix of the three changes (the attribute, the new file, its write), never one out of order.
  - Seen under the throttle: a prefix of 2.

### Review round (2026-11-27, PR 623, Opus reviewer)

The previous engineer's work (uncommitted when its session stopped) was committed as a WIP commit and merged with the epic at
`a9afc56b`. An Opus review found the following. Each was fixed in this PR unless it says otherwise.

- **`BlkRig::release` waited for the wrong claim.** It waited on `blk_unclaimed`, which watches only the scratch disk, so after a
  fault-disk driver the next `spawn_at` could be answered `Busy`. `BlkDriver` now carries its requester id, and the release waits
  for that function (`blk_unclaimed_at`).
- **A failed open after a `Busy` was final.** A claim refused while the old driver's domain or vectors are still being taken down
  (`devmgr` answers `Failed`) is the same "old driver winding down" class as an unreadable probe. `mounts::discovery` now retries
  both within one window from the first failure. Covered by a new host test, and the Kani proof extended to it (still about 4 s).
  `volmgr` also closes a handle that came with an answer it opens again after.
- **The T209 budgets are pinned in the gate.** The first half of `volmgr_retries_a_busy_device_and_publishes_only_mounted_volumes`
  works from time, not from counts. Each disk is answered `Busy` for 4 s, then with a dead driver for 4 s, then for real. The
  volume must be published within `volume_publish_bound()` (the bound `open_volume` uses) and after more than the old 15 s. Each
  disk must have been probed through a real driver once. `volume_publish_bound` now allows one `READY_MS` per disk, since the
  servers start one after another.
- **The T122 half waits for a positive event.** It used to wait a fixed 2 s. It now watches the rig job's process count until the
  volume server has started (and ended), then waits 500 ms, then asserts `NOT_FOUND`. If a start is too short to see, it says so
  and has waited 5 s anyway.
- **No cascade from that test.** Device 1 is formatted back to a mountable volume whatever happened in between.
- **`fsd_keeps_files_across_a_restart` checks more.**
  - Prefix 0 is no longer looser than the old test: the index must hold `dirtag` on `docs` and exactly one more entry, `alpha`
    on `docs/b.txt` at prefix 0 or `late` past it.
  - The commit window is measured from before the sync.
- **The killed-mid-request driver.** The throttle's bucket may drain during a host pause. A read the device let through is now
  read back and the next one queued; the device then holds it.
- **More leak checks.** That driver's job must also come back to 0 objects (its channels and VMOs) and 0 pages after the release
  (T44's leak-check gap, beyond domains and vectors).
- **Small leaks on error paths.**
  - `KillProcsOnDrop` now closes the handles too.
  - The stand-in closes the handles of an answer it could not pass on.
  - `volmgr`'s `start` closes the listener ends it never handed over, and its process handle, when the server does not come up
    or a publish fails (an existing leak, up to six per volume).
- **Harness.**
  - No fault disks and no `OS_DISK_THROTTLE` under record and replay. `blkreplay` reads its image node directly, so a drive's
    throttle would not apply, and the throttle's timers run on the host clock, which replay does not reproduce. The first fix
    moved the throttle below `blkreplay`; the second review showed that does nothing.
  - The required `device 0` line tolerates `, after Busy`.
  - The fault-disk end line is required in the release-content boot too (that build prints the suite's other `s6:` lines).
  - A passing boot prints a `note:` when a volume server took 5 s or more to be ready, with the count of `##PAUSE` lines. A slow
    mount is no longer hidden by the longer wait.
- **Answered, not changed.**
  - T209's strike: the reviewer argued that the cause is not demonstrated, because the slow stage in the two CI sightings is not
    shown. The failure itself is named: a test bound shorter than the server's own, shown by reproducing the same signature with
    a slow disk, and now pinned by the gate test above. What made `fsd`'s mount slow on those runs stays not shown, as written
    above. The new `note:` line and the stage timings make the next one visible.
  - T269's fetch: the 599 ms is unexplained, as written above.
  - `OS_DISK_THROTTLE` on `pd3` (which already has a throttle) is refused by QEMU itself, which is a loud enough error for a
    by-hand switch.
- **Second review round** (the same reviewer, on the fixes).
  - The record-and-replay throttle, as above.
  - A failed open retried after `Busy` is now said once.
  - The driver job's duplicate no longer skips the release when the duplicate fails.
  - Not changed: `BUSY_FOR`/`DEAD_FOR` stay at 4 s against the 5 s windows. A late answer of more than 1 s, at that moment,
    fails the test; it can never pass it falsely. Less margin would not keep the total past 15 s.
