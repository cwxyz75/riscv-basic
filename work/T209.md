title: MM8 left out: decommit and resize of family members, merging a hidden node, PRIVATE after a source copy, the loom test, the ledger for internal VMOs

MM8 (KERNEL-V1-SPEC 4.3) was built in 2026-11-21 except:

1. **`vmo_decommit` and `vmo_set_size` on a member of a family** (a child, the source of a SNAPSHOT, or a PRIVATE source with children) are refused with BAD_STATE. The spec says decommit "frees only that member's own pages" and refuses set_size only for a VMO with children. Why acceptable: a page given back from a member would show the older page of its parent through the hole instead of zeros; the fix needs a per-page "this page reads as zero" marker (a hole entry in the page index) and is part of the pager work (MM9), which needs the same thing. Risk: a fork child cannot decommit what it inherited. Proper fix: hole entries, with MM9.
2. **No merge of a hidden node into its last remaining child.** When a SNAPSHOT leaf dies, the hidden node (and the pages it holds, charged to the source's job) stays until its other child goes too, and lookups keep walking through it (depth stays up). Why acceptable: the memory is accounted (the job pays for what it holds) and bounded by the family; a long chain of fork-and-exit pays for the depth limit (64) with BAD_STATE. Risk: a server that forks in a loop hits the limit after 64 live generations (not 64 forks). Proper fix: merge on the last release (move the node's pages into the child, or the child's into the node when it holds fewer), and the move-instead-of-copy when no other leaf can see an ancestor's page (the spec's "or moves it").
3. **PRIVATE visibility after a source's copy.** A PRIVATE child that has read a page the *source only inherited* maps the ancestor's frame; when the source later copies that page (a write), the child keeps reading the old frame until its mapping is refreshed, where the spec says a PRIVATE child sees later changes. Pages the source owns are seen at once (the frame is shared and written in place). Why acceptable: the use (a private file mapping over a pager-backed parent, MM9) has a source that owns its pages. Proper fix: the source's copy unmaps the page from every mapping of its children (needs the family's mapping index, spec 4.3's "a VMO knows its mappings").
4. **One address space per inheriting VMO.** A VMO that has a parent (or is the source of a SNAPSHOT) may be mapped in one address space only (`process_map` of another is BAD_STATE; a SNAPSHOT of a VMO that two spaces map is BAD_STATE); `vmo_write` and `vmo_commit` on it are BAD_STATE. Why: a copy made through one mapping cannot reach the stale read-only translation in another address space (the fault path holds one address space), and the kernel's own copy cannot either. Risk: a shared-memory server cannot hand a snapshot VMO to two clients; a `fork` shim (one process into one) is unaffected. Proper fix: the VMO's mapping index (spec 4.3), so a copy unmaps and shoots down every mapping.
5. **No loom test** of two leaves write-faulting the same shared page (the item asked for one): the family is under the one VMO lock (`kmem::get()`), so the interleaving it would check does not exist yet; it belongs with the per-family lock of KERNEL-V1-SPEC 4.3 (T204 counts the cost of the single lock). The model test and the QEMU test run single-threaded for the family.
6. **The ledger for internal VMOs.** The hidden node and the child of `process_clone_region` are VMO slots with no kernel object, so the object quota and the kernel-bytes ledger (L5) do not charge them; each is bounded by the depth limit and the region limit (a clone makes one region). Proper fix: charge a family node to the job that owns it as kernel bytes.
7. **The ASID/TLB cost of a replaced translation**: a copy-on-write fault that replaced a translation asks for the whole-TLB shootdown (as every other removal does, TLB2), not one page on the harts that ran the address space.
8. **Fault-around** maps inherited neighbours read-only; a write to each then faults once per page (no batching of copies).
