title: Devices that share a requester id behind a bridge are one domain by rule, and nothing groups or finds them
area: drivers
horizon: someday
risk: M
complexity: H
change_risk: M
refs: [IOMMU-SPEC, OD164, T42, X-BUS, IOMMU8]
---

**What.** IOMMU-SPEC 3.2 states the rule (OD164 point 2): devices that share a requester id behind a bridge are one device id,
so one domain, and a supervisor must claim, isolate and tear them down together. Nothing is built for it: `devmgr` enumerates
bus 0 only, knows no bridge, and has no notion of a group of functions that must go to one driver or one trust level. The
kernel cannot tell either (it knows no bus). The spec also puts out of scope hardware between a device and the IOMMU that lets
a transaction bypass it (peer-to-peer routing below a bridge).

**Why acceptable.** On the reference machine every function is on bus 0 with a requester id of its own; bridges are a non-goal
of the epic (IOMMU-SPEC 14).

**Risk.** On a machine with a PCI-to-PCI or PCIe-to-PCI bridge, two functions handed to two drivers would share one domain
without anyone having decided it (each could reach the other's buffers), or the second claim would fail with BAD_STATE with no
explanation.

**Proper fix, and when.** With the bridge work of topic X-BUS: `devmgr` computes isolation groups while it enumerates (the
functions behind a bridge that takes ownership of requester ids, the functions without access-control services between them),
claims a group as a unit, refuses to split one between drivers, and says so in its listing line; a host model with fake
topologies is the oracle. Do it when a machine with a bridge is a target.
