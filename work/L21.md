## Plan (2026-11-22, phase 6d): four slices, each its own PR on the epic

What exists: `kcore::wait::WaitTable` holds thread registrations `(object, waiter) -> mask`; a port waiter registers on the **port**, whose signals are *derived* (READABLE follows its bindings, which are objects other than the port), so `commit` evaluates every registered port on every commit that touched anything (`derived`), wakes **all** of its waiters when it turns readable (the losers poll and sleep again: T60), a binding records no handle (T61) and `channel_call` waits for READABLE on its channel, not for its reply. W-SPEC sections 3 and 4 say what to build; this is the order.

* **L21a, the exact ready set (no behaviour change).** A reverse index in the registry from a bound object to the bindings that watch it, and per port a first-come list of ready bindings, updated when a *touched* object's signals change (P1: the ready set is exactly the bindings whose `signals & mask != 0` at every commit; the stateless scan the poll does today is kept as the test oracle). The port's READABLE comes from the set, the `derived` closure of `WaitTable::commit` goes, and an unrelated commit costs a sleeping port waiter nothing. Acceptance: a port model (random bind, unbind, signal, close, poll; P1 after every step against the scan), the completeness property already there, and the mutation "drop one touch" must fail it; a benchmark of syscalls with a sleeping port waiter at many bindings.
* **L21b, the wake-one baton (PW).** Per port a FIFO of waiting threads and an `inflight` count: wake the head waiter while `inflight < |ready|`; a woken waiter polls, takes what it finds, and re-queues and sleeps if it finds nothing (never returning empty-handed before its deadline); a killed winner is removed eagerly and the baton passes. The poll reports oldest-ready-first (the report order W-SPEC accepted). Acceptance: W-SPEC 5's port model (PW after every step, fairness bound), the mutations "wake-one without the baton" and "deadline test before poll", loom/shuttle for two waiters, a waker, a timer and a killer, and the QEMU tests for many waiters on one port and the kill of a winner.
* **L21c, binding lifetime (T61).** A binding records the process and handle it was made through and ends when that handle is closed, replaced or sent away (the forced touch the handle table already makes). Acceptance: the model with that step; QEMU tests for close, replace and transfer of a bound handle; the servers that bind and then hand the handle on are the risk and are found by the suite at the epic gate.
* **L21d, `Call{txid}` registrations.** `channel_call` waits for its exact reply (a registration naming the channel and the transaction id), not for READABLE, so an unrelated queued message cannot keep it permanently ready; it is also what L22 (donation through `channel_call`) needs to know which thread serves a call. Acceptance: the `ipc_model` extension (every held call has a live registration), the reply racing a timeout racing a kill under loom, the existing `channel_call_*` suite.

Each slice ends in a Fable review before it merges; `work.py done L21` is the epic's closing commit.
