title: A per-job handle quota (AB1 caps a process at 65,536 handles with a constant)
refs: [AB1, T8]
---

AB1 made a handle table grow on demand with 2^24 - 1 slots, but a duplicate is not an object, so no job quota bounds a table: one process looping `handle_duplicate` could take hundreds of MiB of kernel heap charged to nobody (Fable review of PR 324). The PR caps a process at `abi::limits::MAX_PROCESS_HANDLES` (65,536 slots, under 2 MiB). Why acceptable: bounded and small per process; the process count is bounded by the job's limits. Risk: a legitimate server that needs more than 65,536 handles is refused with NO_MEMORY; a large number of processes each at the cap still adds up. Proper fix: a handle resource in the job ledger (`Resource::Handles`, charged and credited by `insert` and `close`, with a limit in `job_create`/`job_set_limits`), the per-process cap then raised to the encoding's 2^24 - 1, and KERNEL-V1-SPEC 3.2's "bounded by the job's object quota" made true; slots could also be charged as Pages. The slot vector never shrinks and `drain` walks every slot under the object lock: shrink on close of the last handle, and drain in slices.
