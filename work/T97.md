## Shortcut

**`netd` streams (S9 step 4b) deviations and leftovers**:

- (1) [resolved before merge, owner: do it the way stacks do] a posted accept holds no reservation; the buffers are committed when a SYN takes it (`AcceptTaken`), and a SYN takes only accepts the adapter admitted (`refresh_accept_gates`: room in the account and the pool, counted in post order each pass); a connection that finds no room after all is reset and its accept goes back
- (2) the per-connection `GetStats` block for the config class is not built
- (3) [fixed after review] a closed established stream keeps its socket and is drained into the connection until the client's end is empty and gone (20 s at most) before the connection is released as an orphan
- (4) the stack keeps a socket end per stream and drains R into it in 16 KiB pieces, two per stream per pass, so one busy stream gets at most 32 KiB a pass and the rest wait their turn (P = 32 streams a pass)
- (5) `ClearConfig` aborts every connection with reason 4; a link-down alone does not (connections survive a flap, as 5.11 says)
- (6) the reset after a stack restart is the peer's RST for the first segment it sends, so a connection that sends nothing finds out only at its next retransmission or never (no keep-alive).

## Why it was acceptable

Covered by the three QEMU tests (`net_streams_over_loopback`, `net_stream_quotas_and_bad_handles`, `net_streams_survive_a_stack_restart`) at `-smp 4`; the host side of streams (hostfwd, a host server) is step 4c.

## Risk

(1) a listener's backlog is more expensive than the spec says; (6) a silent half-open connection to a restarted stack lingers on the remote side.

## Proper fix, and when

(1) when `netcore` gains an accept gate, or the C library step; the rest with step 8.
