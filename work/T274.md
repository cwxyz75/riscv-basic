id: T274
kind: tidy
title: The IOMMU fence is waited for by polling the command queue, not by the command-queue interrupt
area: drivers
horizon: long
risk: L
complexity: M
change_risk: H
refs: [IOMMU-SPEC, OD166, T42, IOMMU5, IOMMU6]
---

**What.** Every unmap, map, destroy, revoke and move waits for its `IOFENCE.C` by polling the command queue's head with no
lock held, for at most 100 ms (IOMMU-SPEC 6.4; OD166 point 6). The thread spins instead of sleeping, and the command-queue
interrupt source the kernel owns is used only for queue errors.

**Why acceptable.** On QEMU a command completes on the tail write, so the poll is one read. The bound, the leak-and-count rule
on expiry and the lock rule (never held across the wait) make the poll safe; it is only not efficient.

**Risk.** On real hardware a fence may take long enough that a spinning hart shows in latency figures; a slow IOMMU makes every
unmap a busy wait of up to the bound.

**Proper fix, and when.** Ask for the fence's completion interrupt (the fence command's interrupt-on-completion form, with the
kernel's own wired command-queue source: what the IOMMU specification allows is to be checked first) and block the waiting
thread on the ticket, with the fault and command handler waking it; keep the poll for paths that cannot sleep (a teardown under
a lock, the hart-offline path) and keep the same bound and the same leak rule. It adds a wait under the `IOMMU` class's rules
(IOMMU-SPEC 11), so it needs a model with the wake against the timeout, and a Fable review. Do it when a real IOMMU or a
measurement shows the poll costs something. Not before the epic (T42) is closed.
