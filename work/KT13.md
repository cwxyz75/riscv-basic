title: The kernel stack budget: a high-water check in every boot, and bounded recursion
area: testing
horizon: long
refs: [KERNEL-V1-SPEC, OD140]
---

**What.** 16 KiB stacks were sized from the deepest use the suite happened to reach (5.6 KiB). Nothing bounds the worst case.

**How.** (1) Every boot: at thread exit and at the end of the run, each kernel stack's high-water mark (the zero-fill scan `thread::stack_high_water` already does) is checked; above 8 KiB is a `##TEST kernel_stack_budget FAIL` with the thread and the mark. The perturbation, fuzz and allocation-failure runs inherit the check. (2) An audit, recorded in `docs/records/reviews/`: every recursive function and every drop of a recursive structure reachable in kernel context (`PageTable::destroy`, `Registry::sweep`, `BTreeMap` drops, the device-tree walk, formatting) with its depth bound; unbounded ones are made iterative. (3) Large stack frames: `clippy::large_stack_frames` denied in the kernel crates with a 1 KiB threshold.

**Acceptance, written first.** A test thread that recurses to 9 KiB fails the budget check (and one that reaches the guard still reports `##PANIC kernel stack overflow`).

**Review.** Default.
