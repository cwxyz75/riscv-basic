title: The shell test's spawn-rate bound (8 s for 100 spawns) sits inside the spread CI runners now show
refs: [OD140, TESTING]
---

`scripts/s4e_shell_test.py` fails when 100 `xargs` spawns take more than 8 s of guest time (the guard is against the 12.9 s of the old 100 ms timeout). The guest clock is real time (AB2), so a slow runner moves the number: on 2026-11-19 and 20 the same code measured 3.9 and 3.96 s (PRs 281, 282), then 6.46, 8.11 and 6.55 s (PR 283, one of them a failure) and 9.35, 5.73 s (PR 284, one a failure), with no change to the spawn path between them (a re-run of the same commit gave 5.73 after 9.35; locally the test takes 2.97 s). Why acceptable for now: a re-run passes, and the bound still separates the 12.9 s regression from a healthy 3 to 6 s. Risk: a flaky merge gate (OD140 calls it a defect, not noise), and a real slowdown of up to 2x would hide inside the spread. Proper fix: compare against a calibration measured in the same boot (the cost of a fixed amount of guest work, as `BENCH_RATIO` does for the benchmarks), so the bound is a ratio and the spread of the runners drops out; then tighten it.
