### T81: Heap growth frames are unbilled and unreserved

*(area kernel, horizon long, risk M, fixed_by [T203])*


#### Shortcut

**Heap growth frames are unbilled and unreserved** (slice T step 2, Fable review): the grown heap takes frames from the frame database at safe points with no job charge and no reserve, so process memory caps can be exhausted by growth (the caps are not tied to it) and a failed growth with a full heap is a kernel panic route; the window never shrinks

#### Why it was acceptable

Growth is bounded by the caps of OD50 and the job quotas, 256 MiB window

#### Proper fix, and when

Reserve frames for kernel growth in the frame database (or a kernel account), make an empty heap fail the syscall instead of panicking, and shrink after a quiet period

### T203: Heap growth frames and the slab pool are not charged to any job (T81 stays open after MM5)

*(area kernel, horizon long, refs [MM5, T81, T196])*


MM5's acceptance test bounds what a confined program makes the kernel hold in heap bytes (64 KiB over what its job was charged). T81 is the other half: the frames the kernel heap grows by (`heap::grow`, and the slab pool of MM4) come from the frame database at safe points with no job charge and no reservation, so the heap's own growth is the cost of every charged object and is shared by all jobs; a heap that cannot grow is a kernel panic route (an allocation failure inside a syscall is NO_MEMORY, but the boot heap and the grown heap can both be empty). Not fixed by MM5: charging heap growth to the job that caused it needs the allocator to know a "charging job" (MM5's text), which the slab magazines (a free object is not any job's) make a design of its own.

Why acceptable: every object, handle table, register state and queued byte a job causes is now charged and limited (MM5), so a job cannot cause unbounded heap growth; the heap's growth is bounded by the sum of the limits. Proper fix: with T196 (the allocator takes frames inline), account the heap's frames in a kernel pool and reserve headroom for syscalls (a failed growth must not panic); decide then whether the slab's slack is charged.

### T249: The sparse page index is not charged to the VMO's job (T207 PR 1)

*(area kernel, horizon long, refs [T207, MM6, KT9, T177])*


**What.** `kcore::pagemap::PageMap` is sparse (one 528-byte leaf per touched 64-page chunk, class R growth), but the leaves are not yet charged to the VMO's job as `Resource::KernelBytes`, as the T207 plan (section 1) says they should be. The insert sites in `kcore/src/aspace.rs` (`commit`, the fault commit, `pager_supply`, the snapshot copy, `decommit`'s holes) and the frees (`remove`, `release`) would each need the job ledger and a rollback on a refused charge.
**Why acceptable.** Until T207 PR 4 deletes `MAX_VMO_PAGES` a VMO has at most 16,384 pages, so at most 256 leaves (about 135 KiB), where the old outer vector was uncharged too. The VMO count is bounded by the object quota. No new unbounded memory exists yet.
**Risk.** After the caps go, a 2 GiB VMO touched one page per 2 MiB holds 1,024 leaves; many such VMOs would hold kernel heap the job does not pay for. **This item must close before T207 PR 4** (it is T207 PR 2 or 3 work; that keeps it open in the order of the plan).
**Proper fix.** `PageMap::needs_leaf(page)` before each insert: charge `LEAF_BYTES` first, insert, credit back on `NoMemory`; credit `leaf_count()` differences at each removal; credit all leaves at `release`. Teach the J1 ledger audit (`vmo_ops_model.rs`) the KernelBytes term and add the `jobs_do_not_leak` scenario for a sparsely touched VMO. When: T207 PR 2 or 3 (a kernel slice anyway).

#### Plan (Fable, 2026-11-24) and what was built

Mechanism: charge per insert, no counter. `Vmo::put` charges `LEAF_BYTES` to the VMO's job before a new leaf is made (`NoMemory`, nothing changed) and credits it back if the insert fails; `clear_page` / `take_frame` credit by the fall in `leaf_count()`; `release` credits the leaves still held; a device VMO is never charged; a DMA run pays one leaf per 64 pages with its pages. `PageMap` gained `has_leaf` and `clear` (a frame or a hole). Fixed on the way: `supply` took the frame out of the source before it knew the destination could hold it (its rollback comment was false since PR 1: put the destination first, then clear the source), the `decommit` hole rollback used `remove`, which leaves holes, `set_size` kept the holes past the new end, and a merged node's holes were never removed.

Built (2026-11-24): the above in `kcore/src/aspace.rs` and `pagemap.rs`; `Vmos::index_leaves`, `index_bytes_of_job`; `user::job_ledger` leaves out the index leaves of live VMOs (the tests count the VMOs); `vmo_ops_model.rs` asserts the ledger equals the leaves after every step (mutation-checked: no credit in `credit_leaves`, no credit at release); new `kcore/tests/index_charge.rs` (a refused leaf, supply between jobs, a family with holes, shrink, DMA); guest `the_page_index_is_charged_to_the_job` (kernel-only shard); SYSCALLS (`vmo_commit`) and INVARIANTS J1/J2 changed in place. Verified: host tests, k shard at two and four harts, the guest fault-alloc sweep at one hart. One 2-hart run of `a_call_lends_its_priority_to_the_server` failed once with "map a stack: status -4" and passed in the next three runs: watch for it in the epic's smoke.

**Open (why acceptable):** `LEAF_BYTES` (528) is more than the 512 kernel bytes a one-page job gets, so a job limited to one page cannot commit anything (a job of two pages can hold three leaves' worth less one: 1024 bytes holds one leaf). No suite scenario hit it. If a real program needs a one-page job, the one-eighth fraction is an owner decision. `Vmos::take_frames_in` (T207 PR 3) must credit by the difference in `leaf_count()` when it is used by a chunked decommit. Not done: `Vmos::audit_index` as a separate audit (the model test reads `index_bytes_of_job`). Close with `work.py done T249` after the epic's green smoke.

### T201: Page reservations are returned only at teardown, not after 100 ms of idleness; a reservation counts in `job_info`

*(area kernel, horizon long, refs [L16, MM5])*


L16 part d (`kcore::aspace::Reservation`): an address space prepays pages from its job for the fault path (up to 64 at a time, an eighth of the job's room at most, one at least, so a prepayment near a limit cannot starve the job's other users: a 64-page job prepays 8). The prepaid pages count as the job's usage (J1) and come back when the address space is destroyed. KERNEL-V1-SPEC 4.7 also has them returned after 100 ms of idleness. Not built: it needs a timer or a safe-point hook that visits the address spaces that have prepaid pages, which L17's per-process locks give for free (a per-process housekeeping step).

Consequences until then: `job_info` reports usage including the prepaid pages of live address spaces (up to 64 per process of the job, fewer near the limit), so a job's usage can read a little above what its VMOs hold while its processes run; the hog and limit tests tolerate it because the batch shrinks with the room. Proper fix: the idle return with L17, and `job_info` may then report committed and prepaid separately. Risk: a job with many idle processes can have up to 64 pages each prepaid (bounded by an eighth of its room per refill); a process with nothing to fault holds none.

Found in the review of PR 352: the prepaid pages are invisible to the direct charges (`vmo_write`, `vmo_commit`, thread stacks, page tables, `process_create`), so a limited job can be refused early while reservations of its idle processes sit unused: each first fault takes an eighth of the room, so N processes strand about 1-(7/8)^N of it (about 165 of 256 pages for 8). Fixed here: a job with no limit prepays nothing (so the root job's usage stays exact), a reservation holds a use of its job, and a pool that reaches zero lets go of the job. Proper fix with the idle return: give pages back when a direct charge would be refused, so a refusal first asks the job's address spaces to return their prepayments.

### T200: The per-hart frame caches serve one consumer (the slab pool); the rest of the kernel's frame users still take `FRAME` per frame

*(area kernel, horizon long, refs [L16, T196, L17])*


L16 part c built `kcore::fcache` (a cache of 32 frames per hart, refilled and drained 16 at a time under `FRAME`) and `kmem::frame_alloc` / `frame_free_sole`. Only the heap's slab pool uses them. Kernel stacks, page-table pages, user data pages and the VMO commit path still call `db.alloc()` and `db.dec()` under the three memory locks held for a whole syscall, so they gain nothing yet: a user page's last reference cannot be dropped to a cache without the lock anyway (the count is not atomic), and the others need the address-space work of L17 and the inline allocator of T196 to stop holding the locks.

Proper fix: with L17 (per-process address-space locks) and T196 (the allocator takes frames inline), route stacks, page tables and slab frames through the cache; keep user pages on the database (`dec` under `FRAME`). Why acceptable (before L20; the big lock is now the object lock and no longer serialises every taker): the cache's accounting (`free_frames_accounted`) and its model (`kcore/tests/fcache_model.rs`, `fcache_concurrency.rs`) are in place for when it matters. Risk: none now.

Also found in the review of PR 351 (for when the caches get more users):

- **Stranded frames.** Cached frames are invisible to the admission checks that use `db.free_count()` (`aspace.rs`, `pagetable.rs`) and pin order-0 blocks against `alloc_contiguous`; nothing flushes a cache (`FrameCache::flush` has no kernel caller). Near exhaustion up to 32 frames per hart can sit in the caches of idle harts (128 at four harts, 2048 at 64): a commit can fail or the pool report starved while they do. Fix: flush the caches at the idle safe point and before reporting NO_MEMORY, and have admission count `cached_frames()`.
- **The length mirror is not a consistent snapshot** once the big lock stops serialising (it is stored after the refill has dropped the database's count): the leak oracles would be off by up to a batch. Due now that L20 has landed (2026-11-24): take the oracles at a quiet point, or read the database count and the mirrors under `FRAME`.
- Tests: no test frees into a cache other than the allocating one on a second hart's boot, and none exercises misuse; the `UnsafeCell` in `FrameCacheCell` has no Miri coverage.

### T204: Every syscall that takes `kmem::get()` pays for three real locks (the counts baseline went up 13 to 19 percent for it)

*(area kernel, horizon long, refs [L16, L20])*


L16 part b made the VMO store, the job ledger and the frame database three real locks; `kmem::get()` takes all three on every call, whether the syscall touches memory or not (`ksys::Ctx` carries all three, `in_process` takes them for calls that ignore them). The epic gate of phase 6a measured it in the debug kernel at one hart, under `-icount shift=0`: `futex_wake_no_waiter` +19 %, `handle_dup_close` +17 %, `event_signal_wait` +15 %, `page_fault_fresh_page` +13 %, `channel_write_read_64` +2 % (after PR 361 took out the handle-table charging pass, which had been another 1,300 instructions per syscall). `bench/counts.tsv` was re-recorded from CI's numbers of that run (2026-11-21), a decision by the builder; `thread_create_join` fell 10 % and is recorded too.

Why acceptable: the locks are the price of L16 (they nested inside the big lock; the big lock is now the object lock, L20). Proper fix: take only the locks a call needs (`in_process` and the `Ctx` of calls that use no memory take none, and the job-only calls take `kmem::jobs()`), and measure again; a release-build count (`os-ktest-release` is the nearest) is the better yardstick, since the debug kernel inflates lock code. Risk: the 5 % rule hides a further regression up to 15 percent on those syscalls until the baseline is lowered again.
