kind: tidy
title: I/O mappings are 4 KiB leaves only: no superpage I/O mappings
area: drivers
horizon: long
risk: L
complexity: M
change_risk: H
refs: [IOMMU-SPEC, OD166, T42, IOMMU2, IOMMU6]
---

**What.** A device domain's page table is written with 4 KiB leaves only (IOMMU-SPEC 3.2; OD166 point 5 keeps it and calls
superpage I/O mappings a later optimisation). A large buffer (a 64-page pool, a future frame buffer) costs one leaf entry and
one translation-cache entry per page.

**Why acceptable.** Every DMA buffer of the system is small (130 pages for the network device, 4 for a disk), DMA VMOs are not
contiguous any more (OD162), and QEMU's translation cache makes the per-page cost invisible. Correctness does not depend on it.

**Risk.** Performance only: more table frames (charged to the driver's job) and more IOMMU cache misses on real hardware for
large buffers.

**Proper fix, and when.** 2 MiB (and 1 GiB) leaves in `iommucore::iopt` where a mapping covers an aligned, physically
contiguous run: this needs a DMA VMO that can be asked for contiguous frames again (an opt-in flag, not the default), the
invalidation of a superpage range, and the rule that an unmap never splits a leaf (whole-mapping unmap already gives it). It is
a spec amendment (IOMMU-SPEC 3.2, 14) and needs the owner's permission. Do it when a driver with a buffer of megabytes exists,
or when a measurement on real hardware asks for it. Not before the epic (T42) is closed.
