### T169: L13 leftovers: equal-priority balance only at timer events, timer slack not settable from user mode, no RT window entry

*(area sched, horizon near, risk L)*


#### Shortcut

L13b (no tick) leaves: (1) `Sched2::balance` (the share-out of equal-priority queues, T107 (3)) ran at every tick and now runs at timer events only (a slice end or a sleeper's deadline on that hart), so a lopsided queue of equals on a hart that has no event stays lopsided until a thread blocks or the hart's slice rotates; (2) the thread's `timer_slack` (default 50 µs normal, 0 RT) has no user-mode setter (the reserved flags of `thread_set_sched`, SCHED-SMP-SPEC 4.11); (3) the RT window boundary timer entry (4.11) does not exist because RT budgets are not enforced yet; (4) hart 0's clock and the scheduler's first charge start at the first event (benign).

#### Why acceptable

The suite and the smoke gate pass; (1) only affects fairness among equal-priority threads on busy harts, (2) and (3) are features nothing uses yet.

#### Proper fix, and when

(1) give an overloaded hart a slice-end action (balance at the slice end that already exists, and on a mark), with a test on the pure model that equal queues converge without ticks; (2) with the SC/RT syscall work; (3) with RT enforcement. Before L14.

### T170: L13b review leftovers: round-robin build slice arm, the debug watchdog is a 100 ms tick in disguise, acceptance bounds

*(area sched, horizon long, risk L)*


#### Shortcut

From the Fable review of PR 273 (2026-11-19), not fixed there: (1) in the `sched-round-robin` build `RoundRobin::next_event` makes a slice end an event for any queued thread, but `after_enqueue` marks a remote busy hart only for an equal or better thread, so a lower-priority thread queued remotely behind a lone runner arms no slice timer (a user-mode spinner never yields); (2) in debug builds a hart with queued work arms the watchdog every 100 ms and the interrupt runs a full `tick_on`, which repairs a lost slice-end arm or lost poke within 100 ms: the release suite alone can see such a bug, and the `fault-skip-poke` control is weaker than before (it still ends in `starved thread`); a lower-priority thread queued remotely on a lone-runner hart arms nothing, so real starvation there goes unreported unless another hart has queued work; (3) the L13 acceptance "100 µs for an RT thread" is a 4 ms bound (the emulator's interrupt jitter), `wake_before_block_is_not_lost` allows 12 ticks, `harts_come_online` lost its per-hart one-shot check, the idle and lone-runner tests allow a few interrupts and only check hart 1; (4) wakes stamp `runnable_since` from the hart clock: fixed for `wake_inner` (charged first), not for `spawn_on` and `fresh_as`; (5) a lone runner's `slice_left` is drained by `charge` and not refilled until an interrupt (the first equal peer rotates it at once); (6) without `menvcfg.STCE` the first `csrw stimecmp` traps before the readable assert (loud anyway).

#### Why acceptable

Debug-only or emulator-jitter effects; the pure model and the smoke gate pass.

#### Proper fix, and when

(1) ask the class whether a queued thread makes a slice event (a `Class::needs_slice_timer(eff)`), mark on that; (2) make the debug watchdog sample call only `starved` without `tick_on`, and arm it when any hart has queued work; (3) tighten the bounds when a quiet-host soak shows the margin, test every hart; (4) charge in `spawn_on`; (5) refill on idle-to-busy; do (1) and (2) before L14.

### T210: devmgr starts drivers in jobs with a real-time ceiling and a budget (SCHED-SMP-SPEC 4.9, the last line of L8's policy)

*(area sched, horizon long, refs [SCHED-SMP-SPEC, L8])*


**What.** 4.9 says driver jobs made by `devmgr` get an RT ceiling and a budget, so interrupt threads can sit in the real-time band. L8 built the mechanism and the rest of the policy (the launcher's 20 and no budget, the session job's 31); `devmgr` still spawns `blkd` and the other drivers into its own job, which has the root's 63 and the whole window, and no driver runs a thread at a real-time priority.

**Why it waits.** A driver job's ceiling and budget are only meaningful once something enforces the budget (L25) and a driver has an interrupt thread that asks for the band; `devmgr` also has no job handle of its own to make child jobs from (`job_root` goes to `init` and `sessiond`), so the change is a handle hand-over in `init` plus a job per driver. Doing it now would set numbers nothing reads. The risk of waiting: none today (drivers run at the default priority); the root-sized ceiling is the status quo.

**Proper fix.** With L25: `init` gives `devmgr` a MANAGE handle to a `drivers` job below the root with `max_prio` 50 and `rt_budget` 20 ms per window; `devmgr` makes a job per driver below it; the first driver interrupt thread that wants the band (HD items) sets it, and a suite test shows a driver job cannot exceed its ceiling.

### T211: GP1 is excused on a hart whose real-time share is spent (L25): find why a normal thread then waits on another hart

*(area sched, horizon long, refs [SCHED-SMP-SPEC, L25])*


**What.** L25's throttle (NM1) holds a hart's real-time queue back when its share of the window is spent and the normal band has work. The GP1 audit (`kcore/src/sched_smp.rs` `audit`) and the two walk references (`kcore/tests/sched2_smp_model.rs`, `sched2_kernel_walk.rs`) skip such a hart for every waiting thread, not only real-time ones.

**Why it is acceptable for now.** The only violations seen are a normal thread waiting on another hart a little longer than strict priority allows while a hart is in the throttle (never a lost thread, a double dispatch or a real-time thread running past its share: the NM1 and demotion tests check those directly). The Fable review of PR 390 asked for the narrow form (excuse only real-time candidates) and it fails: `gp1_and_gp2_hold_on_random_walks_over_one_to_eight_harts` seed 904 step 331 (hart 3 runs priority 15, thread 66 of priority 19 waits on hart 4, whose queue is led by held-back real-time threads), and seed 964 step 67 without the mid-switch excuse.

**Proper fix.** Find which settle or pull misses it (the pull of the best normal thread behind a held-back real-time queue, or the push offer that should mark hart 3), fix it, and narrow the excuse to real-time candidates. Do it with the next change to placement (SC2) or when the walks are next touched.

### T231: Spread placement can put two new light threads on one hart when the first has already gone to sleep (seen in CI)

*(area sched, horizon long, refs [PM4, PM1])*


**What.** At the phase 10 gate, `kernel (ktest 4 harts)` and `kernel (ktest release)` failed: two light periodic threads created one after the other were taking their wakes on one hart. Spread puts a thread on an idle hart; the first thread, run and asleep before the second was placed, leaves its hart looking idle, so the second joins it, and each then keeps waking on its last hart.
**Why acceptable.** It is the rule working as written (a thread returns to its last hart when it would run there; an asleep hart is idle), and nothing is lost but spread across harts for two light threads. The tests now create the pair with interrupts off and give the spread measurement three tries.
**Risk.** None for correctness.
**Proper fix.** If spread should prefer an unloaded hart to an idle one that already has a sleeper homed on it, `select_spread` needs a per-hart count of homed threads (a hint); decide with the SC2 measurements.

### T83: Task 9 leftovers

*(area sched, horizon long, risk M)*


#### Shortcut

**Task 9 leftovers** (`thread_spawn`):

- (1) `MAX_THREADS_PER_PROCESS` (2048) still exists beside the job's thread limit; SCHED-SMP-SPEC 5.3 removes it once launched programs get their quota (done: 128) and the `thread_limit_and_slots_are_reused` test is rewritten around a job limit.
- (2) Every stack release costs a full shootdown (an IPI to every hart) and `unmap_region` walks every page of the stack: OD93's ranged shootdowns and a pending-set of harts that ran the process would cut it.
- (3) The thread object limit is the handle table: a thread handle per thread, so a 4096-handle process cannot hold more than about 4000 joinable threads (detached threads have none).
- (4) `a1` = tid is set only for `thread_spawn`; `thread_create` and the first thread still start with `a1` = 0.
- (6) the stack arena is not protected from `process_map`/`process_unmap`: a program can map into it or unmap a spawned stack (self-harm only; `free_spawn_stack` unmaps by address, accounting balances)
- (7) `pthread_create`'s handle hand-off (`handle_of`) spins on `thread_yield`, which can livelock under strict priorities if the creator is lower priority than the thread: have the kernel store the handle before the thread runs, or wait on a futex
- (8) guards: the guard above a stack is at least the stack's own guard size, not the neighbour's, when sizes differ
- (9) no test for the other start registers being zero, a cross-process successful spawn, or a stale-TLB negative control (Fable review of task 9, findings 3, 6, 9); (5) `ROUND_ROBIN` is recorded (`thread_get_sched` shows it) but the scheduler has no round-robin class yet.

#### Why it was acceptable

None is a safety problem

#### Proper fix, and when

- (1) with T79's limit review
- (2) with OD93
- (3) with the handle-table work
- (5) with the scheduler class work

### T107: Task 11 stand-ins

*(area sched, horizon long, risk M)*


#### Shortcut

**Task 11 stand-ins** (L11b-1: the kernel runs `kcore::sched_smp::Sched2`, one run queue per hart; Fable review `docs/records/reviews/2026-11-16-l11b-scheduler.md`):

- (1) done by L11 (2026-11-19): per-hart `RQ` locks, `need_resched` (a wake never switches), the dead list in `finish_switch`, the idle loop of 3.9, the starvation watchdog (PRs 262 to 264)
- (2) the `rr` flag of `thread_set_sched` is not passed to the scheduler, so a real-time user thread never rotates (SYSCALLS.md says so). Still open (rechecked 2026-11-24): the kernel stores `sched_flags` but `thread_set_sched` only calls `set_priority` (`kernel/src/user/calls.rs`), and every `SchedAttr` the kernel builds has `rr: false`; the scheduler core does honour `cur.base.rr` once it is set. An earlier note of 2026-11-24 struck this item as done; that was wrong.
- (3) equal-priority threads are balanced only by queue length: a pull needs a strictly better thread, and at a tick a hart takes one equal thread from a queue at least two longer than its own (`Sched2::balance`; it brought the thread-hog test from 5.8 s to 3.8 s at four harts, the shared queue took 1.7 s), so three equal hogs on two harts still get 100%, 50% and 50% of the CPU; fix: the fair class
- (4) a pulled thread keeps its slice but goes to the back of its level (a preempted real-time thread should return to the front)
- (5) hot-path costs that grow with the queue, not the hart count: pull and push walk a hart's queued *pinned* threads (kernel threads are pinned to hart 0) with a map lookup each; `detach_queued`, `is_queued` and `PrioQueues::remove` scan all 64 levels; `RoundRobin::top` is O(queue); records move between maps on every enqueue (allocation in the wake path, X8); measured before the fixes below: `a_thread_hog_cannot_exhaust_the_kernel` went from about 1.7 s to 9.9 s of guest time and `ten_thousand_threads_leak_nothing` from 6.3 s to 8.0 s; fix: a per-hart best-free-priority mask, an index of where each thread is queued
- (6) the round-robin build keeps a strict real-time band (the boot thread at 40 and the reaper at 50 preempt every normal thread), so it proves less than OD32 says
- (7) a timer on a secondary hart fires on that hart's own tick, up to one tick after hart 0's (never early), and a timer-woken thread is queued on its home hart without placement (a push or pull moves it)
- (8) `class_name()` returns fixed strings.

#### Why it was acceptable

A pure model exists for every rule, the whole suite passes at one, two and four harts, and nothing here is unsafe, only slower or less fair than the final design.

- (2) with the RT work (tasks 25, 27)
- (3) to
- (7) when the benchmark (`os-bench`, the lossy relay and the churn test are the load) says they matter.

### T108: Task 12 stand-ins

*(area sched, horizon long, risk M)*


#### Shortcut

**Task 12 stand-ins** (L12 step one: the scheduler behind its own `RQ`-class lock):

- (1) one scheduler lock for all harts, not one per hart (`Sched2` is still one value); every tick of every hart takes it briefly, so it serialises them (shorter than the big lock, not gone)
- (2) a tick or reschedule IPI that interrupts **user mode** still takes the big kernel lock (the return to user mode is where a killed process dies, `activate_current`, which reads state under it; FP state is saved and restored under it too): fix: a per-thread kill flag readable without the lock and FP state in the thread record (T57), tasks 17 to 20
- (3) ~~`thread_yield`, `clock_get` and pure sleeps are syscalls and still take the big lock at the trap~~ (L20, 2026-11-24: a syscall takes only the object lock, per section)
- (4) (review of this step: a wake takes the scheduler lock three times, once to read the state, once to wake, once in `reschedule`; a lock-and-switch-per-hart design would take it once) instead of holding the scheduler lock across the context switch (the spec's `finish_switch`, which would need the thread table under the same lock), a thread just switched away from is marked *in transit* and `Sched2::switched` is called after the switch (TR1); that costs one extra scheduler lock round trip per switch
- (5) `need_resched` (X7), the dead list and reap by the hart, the idle loop of 3.9 and the starvation watchdog are not built
- (6) the idle loop looks at a lock-free flag before taking the big lock for the heap safe point (`heap::needs_service`)
- (7) a debug-build spin watchdog (30 million iterations on one wait) turns a deadlock into a panic naming the class last asked for; not in release builds
- (8) no negative-control *kernel* for TR1 (the race is probabilistic, so a kernel that must fail would flake; the model-test mutations are the controls: pull ignoring transit, transit never recorded, transit overwritten)
- (9) the QEMU test `a_wake_preempts_a_lower_priority_hart` now accepts two thirds of the wakes as IPIs (a secondary's tick may pull the thread first), which no longer detects one lost IPI in twelve.

#### Why it was acceptable

The whole suite passes at one, two and four harts, the model tests check the transit rule and the poke contract, and the remaining big-lock traffic is the same as before this step.

- (3) tasks 17 to 20 and the syscall peels
- (4) to
- (5) when the thread table moves under the scheduler lock.

### T168: AB2 leftovers: ns-grain tests for futex, port and channel_call, the ticks_to_ns shims, hart 0's first clock step, a mixed-clock watchdog

*(area sched, horizon near, risk L)*


#### Shortcut

AB2 (PR 270) moved the ABI to nanoseconds. Left, from the Fable review (2026-11-19): (1) the acceptance asked for 1000 sleeps and checks at nanosecond grain for every wait; the suite has 300 `object_wait_ns` waits of at most 3 ms and a 5 ms check, none for `futex_wait_ns`, `port_wait_ns`, `channel_call_ns` or a `u64::MAX` deadline; (2) `ticks_to_ns` and `sleep_ticks` remain in `thread.rs`; (3) hart 0's scheduler clock stays 0 until its first tick, which then advances by all the time since (`BOOT_COUNT` is set by the first secondary): harmless now, inflates the boot thread's `cpu`, matters once RT budgets are enforced (`set_clock(0, now_ns())` once the timer is running, while never ahead of the real clock); (4) the starvation watchdog samples every 100 ticks (tick count, which lags) but its limit is 500 ms of real time; (5) the C transcript's `clock_gettime` check against a host oracle; (6) the many millisecond bounds in the suite are real time now and about 2.6 times tighter under load than when ticks dilated: the soak run (owed) shows the margin.

#### Why acceptable

The ABI is right and `make smoke` is green; these are test breadth and precision.

#### Proper fix, and when

Do (1) to (3) with L13, which replaces the tick; (4) falls with it. (5) with the next libc work. (6) from the soak result.

### 2. Design, per PR

**2.A PR 1: the scheduler core (kcore, plus two kernel lines). Kernel slice: its own feature PR and a Fable review.**

1. **T169 (1), balance without a tick.** Keep the rule (a hart takes one equal thread from a queue at least two longer) and its mover (the destination, under `lq2`). Add the trigger: in `tick_on`, after the settle, the ticking hart `g` looks at the `queued_len` hints; if an online hart `x` has `len(x) + 2 <= len(g)` and `view_cur(x)` is not above `g`'s best movable queued thread, `mark(x)`. Move the `balance(h)` call from `tick_on` into `settle_with` (after the pull loop), so a marked hart balances when it serves the mark. A hart with a queue of equals always has a slice event, so the offer repeats every slice until the gap is below two. *Rejected:* `g` moving the thread itself (the first move where a hart changes another's queue for a thread it does not outrank; today only the destination moves, which keeps RQ1/TR1 reasoning one-sided); a periodic balance timer on short harts (a tick again, KERNEL-V1-SPEC 5.1); the fair class (T72, `someday`).
2. **T170 (1).** Add `fn slice_event_for(cand: Prio, cur_eff: Prio) -> bool` to `Class` as an associated function (no `self`: `after_enqueue` holds no lock and reads hints), default `cand >= cur_eff`, `RoundRobin`: `true`. `after_enqueue` (`sched_smp.rs:1138-1142`) asks the band's class instead of comparing. *Rejected:* marking on every remote enqueue (an IPI per wake in the default build).
3. **T170 (4).** `spawn_on`: `self.charge_now(target)` before `self.lq(target)` in the loop (the pattern of `wake_inner`; `charge_now` takes and releases the lock itself, so no nesting). Check `adopt_current_on` and every other `fresh_as` caller the same way.
4. **T170 (5).** In `HartRq::charge`: when `slice_left` reaches 0 and the thread's class reports no `next_event` for it (no equal or better peer), refill to the class's slice, as `account` does. *Rejected:* routing `charge` through `account` (it returns "rotate now", which `charge`'s callers do not act on).
5. **T211.** Narrow the excuse at the three sites to `held && band(eff) == Band::Rt`, run the two recorded seeds (904 step 331; 964 step 67) and read the state dump `audit` prints. Hypotheses, most likely first: (H1) `pull_work` computes `mine = cur_eff.max(queued_top())` (`sched_smp.rs:917`), and `queued_top` counts the destination's own held-back RT threads, so a throttled hart running priority 15 with a queued RT 40 refuses a normal 19: use the best queued thread that `dst.would_run`; (H2) `push_marks_with` reads `would_run_hint(x)` for `x != g` (`sched_smp.rs:855-859`), published before `x` entered the throttle; (H3) `balance`'s `p < cur` test has the same blind spot as H1. Fix what the dump shows, keep the narrow excuse. If two days do not find it, put the wide excuse back and add the dump to this section.

7. **Hart 0's clock.** `sched.set_clock(0, now_ns())` in `thread::init` before `adopt_current_on`. The timer must be up first: check the order of `timer::set_frequency` and `thread::init` in `kernel/src/main.rs`; `now_ns` before `MULT` is set returns 0 and the call is then harmless but useless.

### 5. Invariants, locks, and how this goes wrong

- **Rows:** GP1 (PR 1 changes both what satisfies it and what excuses it), TR1 and RQ1 (`balance` moves to `settle_with`: it must keep skipping threads in transit and re-check under `lq2`, as now), ID1 and the poke contract (every new `mark` needs its poke bit: `sched2_kernel_walk.rs` checks it, extend the walk to the balance offer), NM1 (the T211 fix must not let an RT thread run on a spent hart: `sched2_rt_budget.rs`), AC1 (the hart 0 clock and the `spawn_on` charge change who is charged), PK1 and PK2 (untouched, but the walks check them on every step), X7 (a mark never switches: the offer is a mark, nothing more), AF1 (balance respects `allowed`), PR1-PR2 (PR 2 and PR 4), HT1 (no mark for an offline hart: `mark` and `balance` already test `is_online`).
- **Locks:** `RQ` (class 9, per hart; two at once only in ascending hart index through `lq2`), `TT` (8, before `RQ`), `PI` (7, held by `set_priority_held` around the scheduler call: `set_attr_on` keeps that order), `OBJ` (1, the syscall section in PR 2), `JOBS` (6, the `max_prio` read). No new class and no new nesting. `charge_now(target)` in `spawn_on` must not be called with any `RQ` lock held.
- **Subtle failures to look for:**
  1. *Ping-pong.* The offer and the mover read hints; if the offer uses "2 longer" and the mover re-checks "2 longer" under the locks, a thread cannot bounce, but an offer on stale lengths costs an IPI per slice for nothing. Count offers in the convergence test and bound them.
  2. *Balance inside `settle`* runs on every look at the queues, not once per tick: it must stay O(harts) on hints with at most one `lq2`, and must not loop (one thread per settle, as now one per tick). Re-check `pair_scaling` at the epic smoke, since T267 watches the same path.
  3. *A user-mode lone runner now takes an IPI* when a neighbour's queue is two longer. That is the point, but `user_mode_ticks_take_no_big_lock` and the lone-runner interrupt counts (`smp_tests.rs:805-880`) have tight allowances.
  4. *The T211 fix widens what a throttled hart pulls.* If `mine` ignores held-back RT threads, check the moment the window ends: the hart then holds a normal thread it pulled and an RT thread it should run at once; `restore_demoted` and the window-end event must mark it.
  5. *The slice refill in `charge`* must not refill a thread whose equal peer is queued (it would never rotate): the condition is the class's `next_event`, not `slice_left == 0` alone. `tick_starvation.rs` is the guard.
  6. *The watchdog change removes a repair.* Any lost arm or lost poke the 100 ms `tick_on` has been hiding in debug builds becomes a `starved thread` panic at the next smoke. That is a real defect each time (TESTING.md rule 11): expect it, do not put the tick back; this is why PR 3 follows PR 1.
  7. *`rr` on a donated thread:* the class reads `cur.base.rr` with the effective priority; a normal-band thread boosted into the RT band has `rr` false and runs FIFO there. Decide in the PR whether that is right and say so in SYSCALLS.md (4.5 is silent).
  8. *`SUSPENDED` in `pthread_create`:* a thread killed or a process exiting between spawn and resume must not leak the TCB or hang `exit`; a detached create must not take this path.
  9. *Strict `Held::release`* can turn a harmless double release on a panic path into a second panic: keep `Uart` lenient.
