title: MM7 left out: the C library's mmap/munmap/mprotect, the protect-versus-sibling-write negative control, per-job region counts
refs: [MM7, MM6, PL8]
---

MM7 (KERNEL-V1-SPEC 3.5, 4.5) was built in 2026-11-21 except:

1. **The C library**: `mmap`, `munmap` and `mprotect` for anonymous memory are not mapped onto `process_map` (ANYWHERE), `process_unmap` (ranges) and `process_protect` yet (`libc/`, `oslibc`): that is user-space glue over calls that now exist and are tested; the allocator idiom (map twice the size, trim) is tested directly in the suite (`process_protect_and_range_unmap_from_user_mode`).
2. **The negative control** `fault-protect-no-shootdown` and the QEMU test "protect to read-only while a sibling thread writes": the shootdown after a downgrade is in place (`proc_call` asks for it when `ProtectStats::downgraded`) and tested on the host (`ksys/tests/proc_calls.rs`: counts), but no boot shows a stale writable translation on another hart (QEMU's timing, as for T193).
3. **The region count per job** (PL8): `MAX_REGIONS` (16,384) is still the per-address-space limit; the spec has a per-job `regions` level.
4. **ANYWHERE's choice** takes a uniformly random start and the first gap from there, wrapping once, not the spec's "8 random positions then first fit": a start just below a large occupied range is likelier than one in a wide gap's middle, so the placement is not uniform over gaps. Why acceptable: the address is unpredictable to a process that cannot read the pool; the bias is slight. `aslr=off` (first fit) is not built: the pool gives 0 only when unseeded.
5. **The page-table charge of a split mapping** is kept per `process_map` (its start and where it ended: `Process::charge_end`) and credited when no region is left in that span: a mapping that a range unmap or a protect split keeps the whole charge for the part that stays, and a mapping folded into its neighbour keeps its own. The charge can therefore stay higher than the tables that are left (it comes back when the last piece goes or at teardown); it is never lower (the review of PR 370 found the first version credited it early: a bypass of the job's page limit).
6. **`process_map_info`'s VMO field** is the VMO store id, not a kernel object id (a region does not know its VMO object); stable for a VMO's life.
7. **Deviations from KERNEL-V1-SPEC 3.5** found in review: ANYWHERE is triggered by `vaddr` 0 alone (a caller that passes 0 by mistake gets a mapping, not INVALID_ARGS); the `process_map_info` record has six words, the spec's seventh (`flags`) and a VMO koid are missing (the VMO store id is global: an INSPECT holder of a process can learn the ids of VMOs it has no handle to; the id is never a capability); ANYWHERE keeps no guard gap and can land flush against the main stack's top if the child's stack is mapped after it.
8. **Merging at map time** makes `process_unmap(vaddr, 0)` remove a merged mapping whole (documented in SYSCALLS); and a thread's spawn stack is credited and freed by its region start (`free_spawn_stack`): a process that protects or range-unmaps the first page of its own thread stack leaves the rest mapped until process exit (a self-inflicted leak, not a bypass: the VMO stays alive through the split's reference).
9. **Range operations have no length bound**: one `process_protect` or range `process_unmap` walks every page of every region in the range under the big lock, up to the whole user half; the cost is proportional to what the job has mapped. With L19 (chunked, preemptible).
10. **A VMO mapped writable at one address and executable at another** is not synchronised per frame (PL7 syncs when a page becomes executable); M9's "never writable and executable at once" holds per mapping, not per frame.
