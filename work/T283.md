Part of the stability epic (`STAB0`). Moved here from T264 on 2026-11-27: parts T195, T193 and T232 (their text below, as it was).
Added: the `overnight-repeat-smp` target that OD161 asked for and that was not built, and the two-hour cap of the stability epic.

**Why it is first.** FZ1 needs three consecutive green owner-started soak and long runs. Today a soak run on a laptop or a hosted
runner reports failures that are not bugs (T195: fixed host ports collide; benchmark baselines from another machine), and two
negative controls have been vacuous at least once (T193, T232). A green soak that cannot fail, or a red one that is noise, is no
evidence. The epic's owner-run checkpoints (`STAB0`, "Owner runs") need this first.

## The parts, as they were

### T195: `make soak` runs boots in parallel that share host ports and timing, so a laptop run reports failures that are not bugs

*(area testing, horizon long, refs [T178])*


Found by the first full soak (2026-11-21): `make -k soak` ran three boots at a time on a ten-core Mac and eight targets failed that passed when run alone one at a time: `os-boot-test-aia`, `lossy`, `smp`, `latency`, and one `os-boot-test` boot that reported `##HOSTNET tcp_server NOTRUN cannot bind 47802: Address already in use` (the host-side test servers use fixed ports, so two boots running together collide); `realtime_thread_stays_responsive_under_load` (T178) fails under a loaded host by design. `os-bench` also fails on a machine other than the one `bench/baseline.tsv` was recorded on (2026-11-14): its wall-time ratios were 1.2 to 3.0, and the instruction counts of the same build were about 70% above `bench/counts.tsv` (a different QEMU), so the numbers are machine and emulator specific. Fix: give each boot its own host ports (derive them from a per-target offset, or bind port 0 and tell the guest), default `SOAK_JOBS` to 1 and say why in the Makefile and TESTING.md, and let `make soak` print a one-line summary per target at the end; record the baseline together with the machine and QEMU version it came from, and have `bench_compare.py` say so when the versions differ. Why acceptable: the owner runs it knowing this. Risk: a real failure hides among the load failures.


### T193: The shootdown negative control passes on QEMU 11.1.3: it depends on how long QEMU keeps a stale TLB entry

*(area testing, horizon long, refs [T178])*


Found by the first full soak (2026-11-21, run on a Mac with QEMU 11.1.3; CI installs 10.2.1 from Ubuntu). `make os-boot-test-smp-neg` ends in `negative control FAILED: ... os-fault-shootdown ... passed although it lacks the mechanism unmap_while_siblings_touch guards`, in the parallel run and again alone. The control (`fault-skip-shootdown`) skips the remote `sfence.vma`; the guard test (crash mode 40) has two readers on other harts read a page the main thread unmaps and remaps, and fails if one reads the previous round's data through a stale TLB entry. That only shows if the emulated hart's TLB keeps the entry; QEMU 11 evidently drops it (a trap, an interrupt or the ASID handling flushes it) often enough that the test never sees stale data. The mechanism itself is not shown to be broken: the control stopped being able to tell. Proper fix: first run the control on CI's QEMU (10.2.1) to see whether it still fails there; then make the guard independent of the emulator's TLB policy: for example give each reader a long run of reads between the unmap and its next trap, or check through the page-table walk and a reference model (the TLB1 and TLB2 invariants hold by construction in a model of the shootdown, which `kcore` could host), keeping the QEMU test as a smoke. Why acceptable: the shootdown is also exercised by every unmap in the suite and the churn tests at four harts; the control is soak-only. Risk: a real regression in the shootdown is not caught by this test on a recent QEMU. (The `earlyreap` control had the same decay and was made deterministic in the same pass: it now takes the freed stack slot and writes to it.)


### T232: Soak run of 2026-11-23 on main (after phase 10): the negative control of the no-shootdown kernel did not fail, and four jobs ran out of time on hosted runners

*(area testing, horizon long, refs [SC2, KT2])*


**What.** The first soak since phase 8 (run 45164224295, `main` at the phase 10 merge): `normal`, `release` and `lossy relay` passed. Found:
1. `round-robin` and the round-robin leg of `smp` failed `a_call_lends_its_priority_to_the_server` and `pi_futex_bounds_inversion`: both are controls that need a starved low-priority thread, which round-robin does not starve. They were never in `PRIORITY_ONLY` (added with L22, PR 417; no soak ran since). Fixed: `debug_stat` 127 says the class is round-robin and the two controls skip themselves there; checked with `make os-boot-test-rr` (367 tests pass).
2. `negative controls`: `unmap_while_siblings_touch` was reported as passing on the kernel with no shootdown (then the run hung to the 240 s timeout): the control's race did not show on the hosted runner. Reproduced locally; cause: the freed frame came straight back as the next round's page, so the stale translation pointed at the right bytes. The test now keeps the previous round's VMOs open until the next is mapped; the control fails as it should and the normal kernel passes the suite at four harts.
3. `miri` and `AIA, RVA23, no disk, serial` were cancelled at their job limits (20 and 40 minutes; now 60 and 80); `smp` took 18 minutes for the suite at four harts (`a_thread_hog_cannot_exhaust_the_kernel` 35 to 68 s).
**Why acceptable.** Soak only; nothing here gates a merge.
**Risk.** The shootdown control may be vacuous (a regression in the shootdown would not be caught); the time-outs hide whatever those two jobs would show.
**Proper fix.** For 2, rerun the control in a loop (`make os-repeat`) to see how often it is vacuous, and strengthen the test (more touches, retry) if so. For 3, raise `timeout-minutes` on those jobs or split them, then rerun when the owner asks.

**Second soak (run 45199049626, after the fixes):** `miri`, `round-robin`, `normal and fail-demo`, `release` and `lossy relay` passed, and the shootdown control now fails as it should on the runner. The `teardown_with_threads_on_other_harts` control (the kernel with the kernel `satp` switch skipped) was reported vacuous: the guarded test passed on the runner (it passes or hangs depending on timing) and the kernel stalled in `kill_a_spinning_child`, the next test that tears a process down. `negative_control` now accepts a kernel that did not finish the suite after the guarded test passed (the normal kernel finishes it); locally the control hangs inside the guarded test, as before.

**Local run of the rest of the second soak's negative-control job** (the CI job stopped at its first vacuous control): every control passes with the fixes (`make os-boot-test-smp-neg os-boot-test-sched-neg`, 2026-11-23). Two more findings: the kill-IPI control hangs one test early, in `user_threads_run_in_parallel`, which also ends spinning processes, so a control may name several tests (`a,b`); and `interactive_nothing_survives_init` (the shell test, s4e) read the job ledger as soon as the process table was empty, before a dying process's chunked teardown had credited it back (2 processes and 3168 IPC bytes still charged); it failed every time on the development machine and passed on the runner, and now waits for the ledger too, with the same 2000-tick bound. **Still open:** `os-bench` failed in the smp job (`pair_scaling_futex_n1` 24 times its laptop baseline against a limit of 10, with n2 at 2.7 times: timing noise of a loaded runner against a best-case baseline).


**The benchmark failure of the smp job (`pair_scaling_futex_n1`, 24 times):** a local `make os-bench` gives n1 4.3 us (0.26 of baseline) and n2 170 us (8.4 times), the runner gave n1 406 us and n2 55 us: the one-pair, two-pair and four-pair futex numbers swing by 30 times or more between runs, with whether the two threads of a pair share a hart (T231). The soak's `BENCH_RATIO` is 30 now (it catches an order-of-magnitude regression, which is what absolute timings on shared runners can show); the futex pair benchmark should report a median of several rounds or pin the pair, a follow-up.

## Stability epic (2026-11-27)

**Scope (one PR).** T195's fixes (per-boot host ports or port 0 reported to the guest; `SOAK_JOBS=1` by default, with the reason
in the Makefile and `TESTING.md`; a one-line summary per target at the end of `make soak`; `bench/*.tsv` record the machine and
the QEMU version and `bench_compare.py` says when they differ), T193's guard made independent of QEMU's TLB policy, T232's
open benchmark note (the futex pair benchmark reports the median of several rounds, or pins the pair), and the two additions above.

**How to investigate.**
- T195: list every fixed host port (`grep -n 'bind\|port' scripts/os_boot_test.py scripts/*.py init/src/bin/suite.rs`), prove the
  collision with two `os-boot-test-smoke-b` boots started at once (an experiment, under ten minutes), then show it gone.
- T193: first read the shootdown control on CI's QEMU 11.1.3 (the soak of 2026-11-25, run 45526124119, reports it passing as a
  control, that is failing the guard test, after T232's change). Then make the guard independent of the emulator: a reader that
  keeps reading between the unmap and its next trap (no syscall, no timer in the window), or a host model of the shootdown in
  `kcore` where TLB1 and TLB2 are checked by construction, kept beside the QEMU test.
- Measure a control's vacuity, not guess it: `make os-repeat TARGET=os-boot-test-smp-neg N=6` is a soak target (rule 6) and is the
  owner's; the builder runs the single control kernel by hand (`scripts/os_boot_test.py --neg <elf> <test>`, one boot, under
  five minutes) as an experiment, up to five times, and records how often it detects.

**Tools.** The harness and `os_repeat.py`; a `kcore` model of the shootdown (host, loom if the model has threads); no new tool.

**Acceptance (runnable, each run under two hours).**
1. Two network boots started at the same moment on one host both pass (one experiment, under 15 minutes), where today the second
   reports `cannot bind`. A harness self-test pins that two boots get different ports.
2. `bench_compare.py --self-test` covers the machine/QEMU mismatch message.
3. The shootdown guard fails on the `fault-skip-shootdown` kernel in five of five single-control boots by hand, and passes on
   the normal kernel at four harts; if a host model is added, a mutation (drop the remote flush) fails it.
4. `check_docs.py --self-test` refuses a dispatch input that would add a trigger, and accepts the piece inputs; a dry run of the
   new `overnight.yml` input (`gh workflow run ... -f target=...`) is **not** started by the builder (OD142): the PR says so.
5. `make -n overnight-repeat-smp` shows `os-ktest-4`.

## Done in PR PRNUM (2026-11-27), into `epic/stability`

Each part struck on its cause; the manager closes the item at the epic's end.

- ~~T195, host ports.~~ **Cause:** the host fixtures (the TCP server, the scripted DNS server, the HTTP server) bound fixed ports 47802 to
  47804, and slirp turns the guest's 10.0.2.2:port into the host's loopback at the same port, so a second boot on one host could not bind,
  or its guest talked to the first boot's servers. Reproduced 2026-11-27: two `OS_SHARD=b OS_SMP=4` boots started at once on the
  development Mac (QEMU 11.1.3), 2 min: one `##HOSTNET tcp_server NOTRUN cannot bind 47802`, the other `net_tcp_to_the_host_server` failed.
  **Fix:** `scripts/host_fixtures.py` binds each fixture at port 0, one set per boot; the kernel line carries `hostports=<tcp>,<dns>,<http>`,
  which the suite reads as `debug_stat` 15 (`kcore::testlog::host_ports`, host test `kcore/tests/host_ports.rs`). The harness now also
  fails a boot whose network tests ran but whose fetch or host-server test did not reach its fixture (a skip there was silent).
  **Proof:** the same two boots at once after the fix: both pass (2 min; a first attempt had both fail
  `realtime_thread_stays_responsive_under_load`, the T178 bound under two four-hart QEMUs at once, which is T284's subject, and its
  network tests passed); `host_fixtures.py --self-test` (docs check) starts two sets at once and pins distinct ports, each answering.
  **Mutation:** the "before" run is the fixed-port harness itself.
- ~~T195, `SOAK_JOBS` and the summary.~~ `SOAK_JOBS` defaults to 1 (the reason in the Makefile, TESTING.md rule 6 and TEST-TOOLS.md);
  `make soak` is `scripts/tier.py run`: it keeps going after a failure and ends with one line per target (PASS/FAIL, wall time, log).
  `chaos.py`'s default parallelism follows (1).
- ~~T195, foreign baselines.~~ Run files and baselines carry `#@ machine:` and `#@ qemu:` lines (`bench_compare.py --provenance`; every
  bench target starts its run file with them, `os-count-record` keeps them); `bench_compare.py` prints a note when the run's machine or
  QEMU differs from the baseline's, or the baseline names none. `bench/counts.tsv` now names its source (CI, QEMU 10.2.1); the two
  laptop baselines say the machine was not recorded. `bench_compare.py --self-test` covers each message.
- ~~T193.~~ **Cause:** the same as T232's second finding (PR 470, 2026-11-23): the previous round's frame came straight back as the next
  round's page, so a stale translation read the right bytes. T193 was seen on 2026-11-21, before that fix; the QEMU version was not
  the cause. **Shown:** the `fault-skip-shootdown` kernel by hand, `OS_SMP=4 os_boot_test.py --neg ... unmap_while_siblings_touch`, five
  boots one at a time on QEMU 11.1.3 (CI's version since 2026-11-26), 238 to 242 s each: detected 5 of 5 (runs 3 to 5 report round 2 of
  300; runs 1 and 2 predate the harness printing the round). The normal kernel passes at four harts (`make os-ktest-4`, and the two
  shard-b boots above). **Independent of the emulator:** `ksys/tests/shootdown_model.rs` (feature gate) runs the rounds of the three
  sibling tests (unmap, protect, decommit; 40 seeds of 60 rounds) on harts whose TLBs keep every translation until the call flushes, and
  checks after each call that no hart keeps a translation the table lost (TLB2, SMP5); its control (a machine whose remote flush does
  nothing) must be caught and is. **Mutations:** dropping `machine.shootdown()` from `process_unmap_step`, from the protect step and from
  the decommit section each fails the model test (round 1 to 3 of seed 1).
- ~~T232, the futex pair benchmark.~~ `pair_scaling_<kind>_n<N>` is now the median of five releases of a fifth of the round trips each,
  with a `ps:` line giving the fastest and slowest release (one release swung by 30 times with the pair's placement, T231). Not measured
  at length: `os-bench` is soak, the owner's.
- ~~OD161's repeat target.~~ `overnight-repeat-smp` repeats `os-ktest-4`, `N` times (20 by default); `make -n overnight-repeat-smp` shows it.
- ~~Two-hour pieces.~~ `soak.yml` takes a `target` input, `overnight.yml` `target`, `chaos_seeds`, `n`, `pkg` and `shard`; a `piece` job
  (120 minutes) runs `make soak-piece` / `make overnight-piece`, which refuse a target outside the tier and malformed values

  plants 17 workflows (extra triggers, a stray or required input, a piece without its limit or guard, an input in a script's text, ...)
  and refuses each, and accepts the real ones. **No dispatch was started** (OD142): the dry run of `gh workflow run overnight.yml -f
  target=...` is the owner's.
