## Shortcut

**`tar` is BusyBox's, not GNU tar** (2026-11-17): the applet is built (create, extract, list, `-C`, `-T`, long options, user and group names); `tar -z` and `-j` are not (no BusyBox `gzip`: pipe through the GNU `gzip` port instead), and the archive is not padded to 10240 bytes (GNU readers accept that). **GNU tar 1.35 was measured and not ported**: it needs about 130 gnulib modules; against our headers 60 of the 120 tried did not compile (no `error.h`, `uchar.h` and the `c32*` functions, `alloca.h`, `getopt.h`, the `*at` calls such as `fstatat` and `linkat`, `timezone_t`), and it has no way to run its own `configure` for the target. ## Why it was acceptable

MAP_PRIVATE` is now served from the heap; file mappings, `MAP_FIXED`, partial `munmap` are not); `chdir` opened the directory for reading only, so relative creates failed after `cd` (it now asks for all the rights, and falls back); `fstat` on a descriptor opened for writing needed read access (`tar` does it on its archive).

## Risk

A working `tar` with the host as oracle (the host's `tarfile` reads our archive), and the libc fixes.

## Proper fix, and when

A GNU tar port needs a libc with `error.h`, wide characters (`uchar.h`), `*at` calls and a generated `config.h`: a slice of its own. **Re-measured after the libc batch (T147, 2026-11-17):** the survey now finds only gnulib's own `localcharset.h` undeclared for GNU tar (`fscanf` and `sigwait` were added the same night) (22 functions and 5 headers before); the 120-module compile attempt has not been repeated, and the generated `config.h` (and a decision about running `configure`) is what remains.
