title: MM6 left out: byte-sized VMOs, no size cap, a VMO's list of its mappings, 2 GiB mappings
refs: [MM6, L19, PL8]
---

MM6 (KERNEL-V1-SPEC 3.5, 4.3) was built in part (2026-11-21): `vmo_create` takes `flags` (RESIZABLE), `vmo_decommit` and `vmo_set_size` exist, mappings are cleared in every address space and every hart is shot down before a frame is freed, a shrink under a mapping ends in a clean fault. Left out:

1. **Byte sizes**: VMO sizes are whole pages (`vmo_create(pages)`, `vmo_set_size(bytes)` takes a page multiple); the spec has `vmo_create(bytes, ...)`.
2. **No cap**: `MAX_VMO_PAGES` (16,384 pages) and `MAX_REGION_PAGES` (1 GiB) stay. Removing them needs region unmap and teardown chunked with preemption (a 2 GiB region is half a million page-table entries cleared inside one big-lock section), which is L19 (the big lock's end). PL8 removes the constants.
3. **A VMO's list of its mappings**: `vmo_decommit` finds the mappings by walking every process's regions (`AddressSpace::unmap_vmo_pages`, O(processes times regions) under the big lock) instead of the spec's per-VMO list, ordered by process id with one address-space lock at a time. Why acceptable: the big lock serialises it and processes are few. Proper fix: the list, with L17's address-space locks.
4. The QEMU acceptance at `-m 4G` (a 2 GiB VMO sparsely touched), the sibling-thread decommit race with its negative control `fault-decommit-no-shootdown`, and the C library's `malloc` returning memory with decommit: need 2 and the allocator's change in `libc`.

Why acceptable: the calls programs need (return memory, resize) exist and are tested on the host and in the suite; nothing relies on the missing parts yet. Risk: a decommit racing a touch on another hart is protected by the big lock and the shootdown order only (no negative control until 4).
