title: Two failures seen once each on an overloaded host: a starvation-watchdog panic and `kill_a_process_that_is_creating_threads` (not reproduced)
refs: [T178]
---

Seen once each during the first run of `make os-chaos` on 2026-11-21 (a Mac with ten cores, QEMU 11.1.3), when the machine was heavily oversubscribed: three chaos boots at a time at up to eight harts each (up to 24 emulated hart threads) beside builds and other boots. (1) seed 2 at eight harts: `##PANIC ... starved thread 2020: queued on hart 3 for more than 500 ms while hart 4 idles (per hart: ...)` (the starvation watchdog of L11d, `kernel/src/thread.rs`). (2) seed 4 at eight harts: `kill_a_process_that_is_creating_threads` failed with `the process ended: got -12, want 0`, which also failed `init_runs_in_user_mode_and_passes`. Neither reproduced: the same two seeds at eight harts passed four times (twice each, one run alongside ten repeat-smp boots), and the restarted chaos run (150 boots, seeds 1 to 50 at two, four and eight harts, two jobs) had no failure but the load-sensitive `realtime_thread_stays_responsive_under_load` (T178). The likely cause is host starvation of an emulated hart (a hart whose host thread is descheduled for over 500 ms cannot run the thread queued on it, and the watchdog sees an idle peer), but that is a reading, not a proof: TESTING.md rule 8 makes an intermittent kernel failure a defect. What to do: make the watchdog robust to a hart that did not run (compare against the hart's own `timer_ticks` or a progress counter, so a hart that made no progress is not blamed on the scheduler), or show the failure by a deliberate host stall in a run; read what `-12` is for the kill test (a status or an exit code) and whether a thread-creation failure under a kill can end the process with it; and run the chaos kernel at eight harts on a quiet machine for a few hundred seeds when the owner next runs the overnight tier. Why acceptable: not reproduced in 154 later boots. Risk: a real rare race in the starvation detector or the kill-while-creating path.
