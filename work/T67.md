## Shortcut

**The network server (`netd`) is one thread with every connection a state machine, not a thread per connection** (`NET-DESIGN.md` section 8). Owner decision 2026-11-14: leave it for now, and reconsider.

## Why it was acceptable

Thread creation is cheap now (`thread_spawn`, thousands fit), but running many threads cheaply needs wake-one on ports (task 21), per-hart run queues and the scheduler out of the big lock (tasks 11, 12) and direct hand-off (task 24), all after S8. A thread made inside `netd` is also **charged to `netd`'s job (about 25 to 30 KiB each), not the client's**, which breaks the client-pays rule (OD59, OD74). Isolation comes from accounts and fuzzed, model-tested state machines instead.

## Proper fix, and when

At S9, once tasks 11, 12 and 24 exist and the stack's own benchmark gives numbers, reconsider **one thread per connection** or, to keep client-pays, **front-end loopers (`rt::looper`) in client-charged threads** in front of one core thread (the core's events-in, actions-out interface was kept so its tests carry over). Measure first, and weigh against the shared-state cost. `S9-SPEC.md` (draft) keeps one thread (its 7.2) and supplies the benchmark (its 14.3).
