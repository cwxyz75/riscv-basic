Part of the stability epic (`STAB0`). Moved here from T254 on 2026-11-27: parts T172 and T173 (their text below, as it was). It is
PR A of T254's plan (2026-11-24): its design, the order of the work and its acceptance are in that plan, section "Design, per
open part" (T172, T173) and the acceptance table's row A, and are restated below so this item stands alone.

**Why it is a stability item.** The stack budget check is what turned the iommu-kernel epic's smoke red on 2026-11-26 (9352 >
9216 bytes in the release build): every epic that grows a syscall can trip it, and a path the suite does not take can be deeper
than any boot has measured. OD152 (3): T172 closes at zero `KT13: audited` allows outside boot and test code.

## The parts, as they were

### T172: 37 kernel functions have a stack frame over 1 KiB (KT13 allows them, audited)

*(area kernel, horizon near, refs [KT13, KERNEL-V1-SPEC])*


KT13 denies `clippy::large_stack_frames` at 1 KiB (`kernel/clippy.toml`) in the kernel crate. Thirty-seven functions already exceeded it, mostly the syscall layer (`syscall`, `process_start`, `channel_call`, the `process_create` closure: up to about 5 KiB in the debug build's MIR estimate), the self-tests, `kmem::init` and the kernel-half snapshot (`[u64; 256]` arrays). Each carries `#[allow(clippy::large_stack_frames)] // KT13: audited`. Why acceptable: the boot-time high-water check (`kernel_stack_budget`, budget 8 KiB of the 16 KiB stack) measures the real depth of every kernel thread in every boot, and the deepest use so far is well inside it; the lint's estimate counts every local at once, which the compiler's frame layout does not. Risk: a path the suite does not take could be deeper than the check has seen. Proper fix: shrink the large locals (box or reuse the arrays, split the big functions), starting with the syscall dispatch path, and remove each allow as it goes; `grep -rn 'KT13: audited' kernel/src` lists them. With TS3c, which moves most of the syscall frames out of `user.rs`.


### T173: The optimised kernel's deepest stack is the reap on a fresh thread (9.4 KiB); bring it under 8 KiB

*(area kernel, horizon near, refs [KT13, T172])*


KT13's boot-time budget (8 KiB of the 16 KiB kernel stack) found, in the release build, that 1205 of the "user" kernel threads in the suite sit at 9352 bytes of high water, and the rest at 7040: not the syscall paths (the stack depth at syscall entry is only 656 bytes) but what runs before the thread's first line, `thread_bootstrap`: `switch_done`, then `reap_prev` under the big kernel lock, which frees the previous thread's stack and bookkeeping (stack pages and their page tables, the run-queue records, the `BTreeMap` removals) on the fresh thread's stack. Under optimisation the inliner merges those frames. Measured by printing the high water at the entry of `user_thread_main`, `syscall` and `teardown`. Why acceptable for now: optimised builds are held to 9 KiB (`thread::STACK_BUDGET`, cfg `kernel_optimized` from `kernel/build.rs`), the debug build to 8 KiB, and the guard page catches an actual overflow; 12 KiB still leaves a quarter of the stack. Risk: the reap path is the one place where the deepest known chain sits under a trap's frames. Proper fix: do the reap from a shallower place (the idle thread, or split `kstack::free` and the table removals into non-inlined steps with the large locals boxed: `kstack.rs:108` is one of the T172 frames), then drop the optimised budget to 8 KiB and delete the cfg.

**Update (2026-11-24).** `reap_prev`, `reap_if_dead`, `note_stack_use` and the drop of the reaped record are now separate non-inlined frames: the fresh thread's high water in the release build fell from 9352 to 8248 bytes, and the optimised budget is 9 KiB. Still to find: the remaining 56 bytes over 8 KiB (not in the reap chain itself; look at `switch_done` and `thread_bootstrap`), then drop the budget to 8 KiB and delete the cfg.

**Update (2026-11-26).** The epic smoke of `epic/iommu-kernel` (run 45906519497) failed the release budget at 9352 > 9216. The deepest path was a syscall, not the reap: `process_kill` through `process_call` (a 4.4 KiB frame, every process call's locals folded in) and the kill's wake, with `user_trap_inner` (576 bytes) no longer inlined into the trap body after IOMMU6/7 grew `syscall()`, and the interrupt frames 512 bytes deeper since IOMMU4 put a 256-byte `IdSet` on the trap stack (twice). Fixed in the smoke-fix PR: `process_call` is a thin dispatcher (each call `#[inline(never)]`), the device-domain arms of `syscall()` are one helper, and the claimed-identity set lives in the hart's record (`PerHart::claimed`, atomic). The deepest use in the release suite fell to 8232 bytes; the budget is still 9 KiB, and the T172 work below (the allows, the 8 KiB budget, the cfg) is unchanged.

## Stability epic (2026-11-27)

**Scope (one PR).** T254's plan, T172 steps 1 to 4 and T173 route (a): shrink the syscall path's large frames (move the body into
an `#[inline(never)]` helper so the big frame is not live across the deeper call, or `FBox::try_new` the local on a class R path;
never box on a class N path), mark boot and test allows `// KT13: boot or test only, on a stack nothing else is under`, add
`ksys/clippy.toml` and `kcore/clippy.toml` with KT13's threshold (their frames run on the same kernel stack), find the last bytes
of the fresh thread's reap chain by measuring, then `STACK_BUDGET` = 8 KiB in both builds and the `kernel_optimized` cfg deleted.
(The log line "deepest kernel stack use among ended threads" includes the deliberate recursion test's exempt thread, which is why it
reads 10336 while the check passes: make the line exclude the exempt thread or say so.)

**How to investigate.** Measure, do not guess: print `current_stack_use()` at the entries of `switch_done`, `reap_prev`,
`user_thread_main` and `syscall` in one release boot (`make kquick`-style, four harts); `-Z emit-stack-sizes` on the nightly
toolchain gives every function's frame from the compiler, and a small script over it and the call graph bounds paths the suite
does not take (a static analysis; `cargo-call-stack` would do it whole but is a new tool: OD, `STAB0`).

**Acceptance (each run under two hours).** `STACK_BUDGET = 8 * 1024` first: `os-ktest-release` (one targeted boot) names the
offender today and passes after; the count of `KT13: audited` outside boot and test code is zero and pinned by the docs check (a
new allow fails it: mutation-checked by adding one); clippy's `large_stack_frames` runs on `ksys` and `kcore`. Review by Opus.

## Done in the T289 PR (2026-11-28), and what is left

**Done.** `STACK_BUDGET` is 8 KiB in both builds and the `kernel_optimized` cfg is gone (the release kernel-only boot already passed at 8 KiB
before any frame was touched: the earlier T173 and smoke-fix work had brought the fresh thread under it). The high-water line leaves out the
exempt deep-stack thread. The kernel crate has no `KT13: audited` allow: every production frame clippy measured over 1 KiB was split into
`#[inline(never)]` steps or had its large local moved (the syscall router, `dispatch_in`, the process and thread calls, the waits, the user
fault path, `user_thread_main`, `teardown_memory`, `leave`, `state()` whose first build is now `fresh_state` from `kmain`, the FP
bookkeeping now a compile-time static, `kernel_half_unchanged` compared entry by entry on every process load, the boot arguments and seeds
found once at boot by `dtb::init_chosen` instead of a tree parse on `debug_stat` and `resource_dtb`); boot and test code is marked
`KT13: boot or test only, on a stack nothing else is under`. `ksys` and `kcore` have `clippy.toml` and deny the lint; `scripts/stack_allows.py`
(docs check, self-test) pins all of it. `scripts/stack_depth.py` bounds the paths no boot takes from the linked kernel (TEST-TOOLS.md).

**Left (48 frames in `kcore` and `ksys`, the lint's estimate over 1 KiB).** Each carries `KT13: audited, clippy's estimate over 1 KiB, left
by T289` and their number is pinned (`AUDITED_LEFT` in `scripts/stack_allows.py`: a new one fails the docs check, one shrunk lowers the pin).
The estimate is the sum of a function's MIR locals at `mir-opt-level=0`; their compiled frames (`stack_depth.py`) are far smaller except a
few (release: `ksys::thread_spawn` 1184, `ksys::create::process_create` closure 1344, `ksys::proc::vmo_range_all` 1264, `iommu::call` 1632 in the
kernel, under the estimate). The proper fix is the one used in the kernel crate: split each into steps of under 1 KiB, or move the large local
out of the frame (ksys's `Reply` and `?` temporaries, the `[u64; 6]` argument copies of each dispatcher arm, `kcore::aspace`'s fault and
range paths). Why not done here: T289 split about 120 functions; these 48 are the rest of the same mechanical work, and the session ran out of
room for it, not of a reason. Risk while they stay: a debug-build frame of these functions can be over 1 KiB on a syscall path; the boot-time
budget (8 KiB, both builds) still measures every path a boot takes.
