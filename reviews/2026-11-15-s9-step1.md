# Review: S9 step 1, the stream data-path prototype (PR #144, branch `s9-step1`)

Independent review with fresh context of `git diff main...s9-step1` against `S9-SPEC.md` section 4 (4.4, 4.6, 4.7), 6.4,
7.2 and 8.3, `SYSCALLS.md` (sockets, ports, jobs and limits), `INVARIANTS.md` and the kernel code the tests exercise
(`kcore/src/ipc.rs`, `kcore/src/object.rs`, `kernel/src/user.rs`). Scope: `init/src/stream_proto.rs`, the socket hand
(`init/src/bin/crash.rs:986-1074`), `S9-STEP1-RESULT.md` and the one-line edit of `S9-SPEC.md` 4.7.

What was run: nothing. Every finding comes from reading the tests against the kernel code; the suite was not booted
and the `##PROTO` numbers in the result were not re-measured. No file was changed except this one.

## Verdict

The central claim holds and the test for it is sound: P1 really can tell the creator from the holder and from the
writer (the only assignment that gives 128 KiB / 0 is "the creating job pays for both buffers"), and the kernel code
agrees (`ipc.rs:719-720`, `785`, `797`). P3 and the rights half of P6 are also what they say. Nothing the tests
exercise contradicts `INVARIANTS.md`.

The written result is stronger than the evidence in several places. The gate was defined as P1 to P8 and P8 was
dropped without a word (finding 1). Two of the numbers that feed 6.4 are off by a factor of two in the optimistic
direction (2, 3). The P4 throughput figure measures the helper's 1 KiB loop, not the pair (4). P2, P5 and P7 each
pass for reasons weaker than their names (5 to 8). None of this overturns option 1; it does mean "the recommendation
stands" should be read as "P1 and P3 confirmed, the cost questions (P4, P7, P8) only partly answered". Findings 1 to
4 should be fixed in the result text (or the measurements done) before the owner decides on it; the rest can be fixed
or recorded in `TIDY-UP.md`.

## Findings

1. **High (result). P8 was not built or measured, and neither the result nor the spec edit says so.** `S9-SPEC.md:56`
   defines step 1 as "the measurements P1 to P8" and 4.7 lists P8 (the bystander cost of 64, 256 and 768 sleeping
   one-shot bindings, T60) with a consequence attached: "If it is large, task 21 moves ahead of step 4 or the limits of
   section 12 shrink." Risk 2 (`S9-SPEC.md:1261-1265`) says "P7 and P8 measure it". The diff has no P8 test, the
   result's "Not measured" list does not mention it, and the spec edit reads "Done 2026-11-15: P1 to P7 pass ... the
   recommendation of 4.4 stands". The result also concludes from P3 that "the per-stream binding count in 7.2 can stay
   at three or fewer", which is exactly the quantity whose system-wide cost P8 was to price.
   Fix: either measure P8 (the `channel_call_rt` benchmark already takes bystanders) or state in both files that P8 is
   outstanding and that the decision about task 21 and section 12's limits is still open. Do not mark the gate done
   until one of the two is true.

2. **Medium (result). "About 250 streams" with 512 objects is twice too many.** P7 counts empty socket *pairs* (127 in
   256 objects, `stream_proto.rs:587-600`). A stream in the proposal costs four objects on the client's job, a channel
   pair and a socket pair (`S9-SPEC.md` 4.5, 6.4 "uses 4 of its 256 objects"). So the launcher default is about 63
   streams and 512 objects is about 127, less whatever the program itself holds. `S9-STEP1-RESULT.md:24` says "about
   250 streams".
   Fix: correct the figure; better, make the hand create a channel pair with each socket pair so the test prints the
   number the spec uses.

3. **Medium (result). The stalled-stream counts are for one direction only; the worst case is half.** P7 fills only
   the direction toward the suite (`stream_proto.rs:615`, 64 KiB per stream), so it finds 3 streams in 192 KiB and the
   result extrapolates "16 stalled streams" for 1 MiB and "32 such streams" for the 2 MiB system budget. But P1 shows
   one pair holds 131,072 bytes when both directions are full, and 3.3, 4.3 and 6.4 of the spec already count 128 KiB
   per connection. A client can fill A by itself (it writes while S is full) and a remote sender fills B, so the
   honest worst case is 1 stream (not 3) under the launcher default, 8 under 1 MiB, and **16 connections for the whole
   system budget**. With the proposed 1 MiB per network program, two programs reach it, as 6.4 says; the result's
   "32" understates how near that is.
   Fix: state both figures (64 KiB and 128 KiB per stream), or have P7 fill both directions.

4. **Medium (result). P4's "16 KiB chunk" figures (12 MB/s) measure the hand's fixed 1 KiB loop, not the pair, and
   "never a wake per byte" is true by construction.** Only the suite's side uses `chunk`; the hand always writes in
   1 KiB pieces (`crash.rs:1032-1047`) and always drains in 1 KiB reads (`crash.rs:1058-1066`). 1 MiB therefore costs
   1,024 hand syscalls, about 66 ms at the result's own 65 µs per call, which is nearly all of the 83 ms and 84 ms
   reported. With 16 KiB on both sides the figure would be several times higher, so the row cannot be used for 4.6's
   "do the copies dominate" question or for a comparison with a ring. Likewise the writer never writes less than
   1 KiB, so a wake per byte (4.6's pathological case) could not have appeared whatever the kernel did; and at one
   hart the reader's single wake per round is the scheduler letting the writer finish, not a property of the socket.
   The result's own caveat ("when the writer was a whole write ahead") covers the second point but not the first.
   Fix: give the hand's `W` and `R` a piece size from the command, run 16 KiB against 16 KiB and a 1-byte writer
   against a blocked reader, and reword the conclusion to what was measured.

5. **Medium (test and result). P5 does not kill "mid-transfer", and its ledger check is satisfied by the suite's own
   reads.** The client is killed while idle in `object_wait` on its bootstrap channel, after `W` has completed and
   replied (`stream_proto.rs:412-418`); nothing is in flight, no thread is inside `socket_write` or blocked on the
   pair. The IPC ledger reaches 0 because the suite then reads all 64 KiB and closes its end (`426-444`): that is the
   credit-on-read path, and it would reach 0 the same way if the client were alive. The case the proposal most needs,
   bytes queued toward the client (store B) when the client dies, is not in P5 (P1's "other exits" step covers the
   credit-on-destroy path, with an upper bound only: see 9). The spec's condition "both jobs' ledgers return to their
   start" is checked for one job; the server here is the suite in the root job.
   Also, the first `job_use_settles(j, 4, 0, 2000)` at line 445 can never succeed, because the suite still holds
   `hand.proc` (the comment at 446 says so): the test always burns its full 2 s before the fallback runs. In a
   smoke-gate test that is 2 s per boot for nothing.
   Fix: kill the client while it is blocked or looping in a transfer (a `W` larger than the buffer with a wait, or a
   reader blocked on an empty end), queue bytes in both directions, check the ledger after the close without
   draining in one variant, close `hand.proc` before the object check, and say "killed while idle" until then.

6. **Medium (test). P2's attempt count is the test's own arithmetic, and the rule it stands for was not exercised.**
   The loop at `stream_proto.rs:222-234` sleeps 20, 40, 80, 160, 320, 500, 500 ms: eight iterations in 2 s whatever
   the kernel does. "8 attempts in 2 s" and the `3..=60` bound (line 237) can only fail if `nap` is broken. What P2
   does prove is the useful part: NO_MEMORY with WRITABLE still set (213-220), persisting, another job unaffected,
   and recovery after the client reads. What it does not show is the thing 4.6 asks about ("a spin under quota
   pressure that the timer does not cure"): no port loop, no one-shot WRITABLE binding next to a timer deadline, no
   `rt::service` server as 4.7 specified ("a throwaway server on `rt::service`"). The suite plays the server with
   blocking calls throughout, so the one-shot-plus-timer rule is still reasoning, not a result. The result's "Not
   measured" list mentions wake counts with a second thread but not this.
   Fix: say so in the result; or drive e1 from a port with the two one-shot bindings of P3 and a deadline, and count
   `port_wait` returns over two seconds (that number is a measurement).

7. **Low (test). P2 does not pin that NO_MEMORY came from the job quota rather than the system budget.**
   `socket_write_in` returns NO_MEMORY for either (`ipc.rs:780-790`). The test accepts any `0 < n1 <= 40 KiB`
   (`stream_proto.rs:213-216`); a system budget exhausted by the suite's other servers would pass with a smaller
   `n1`. The later 64 KiB fill of the rich client (241-243) makes that unlikely but is two seconds later.
   Fix: `ensure_eq!(n1, 40 * KIB)` and `job_use(poor, 5) == (40 KiB, 40 KiB)` right after the fill.

8. **Low (test). P7 has no pass condition.** It only prints (`stream_proto.rs:600`, `624-627`); the loops end on any
   non-zero status without checking it is NO_MEMORY, and on arbitrary caps (300, 10). It is in `REQUIRED` as a
   passing test and the result presents it as a "check". A regression in the object or IPC quota would not fail it.
   Fix: assert the stopping status is NO_MEMORY and pin the two numbers (127 and 3 are deterministic), or list it
   with P4 as "numbers, no threshold".

9. **Low (test). P1: small gaps between the test and what the result and 4.7 say.**
   (a) The result says "exactly 131,072"; the test accepts up to 8 KiB more (`stream_proto.rs:129-136`). The charge is
   exact in the kernel, so `ensure_eq!` would pin it.
   (b) After the other side exits, only an upper bound is checked (line 142); the comment "the creator's own
   direction stays" is not tested. A kernel that freed both buffers would pass. Add `u >= c0 + 64 KiB`.
   (c) 4.7 asks for the check "after the client's duplicate was closed"; there is no `handle_duplicate` anywhere in
   the file. There is also no measurement before the hand-over, so "the charge stays with the creator after the end
   changed hands" is one observation after two transfers, not a before/after comparison. Sufficient for the claim,
   but the wording is stronger than the test.

10. **Low (test). P6 tests the function, not the cases a hostile client would use.** "The same socket twice" passes
    the same handle value twice (`stream_proto.rs:521-526`); a real client sends a *duplicate* (default socket rights
    include DUPLICATE). The koid comparison does catch that, but the test does not show it. Not covered at all, and
    not refusable with `object_info` alone: the client keeps a duplicate of the end it handed over and later reads
    from it (takes bytes meant for the stack) or calls `socket_shutdown` on it, after which every stack write is
    BAD_STATE for good. Both hurt only that client's stream, but the stack must treat BAD_STATE from `socket_write`
    as "abort this stream", and 8.3 does not say so. Two smaller points: `admit` makes and destroys a port per call
    (an object and a binding on the *server's* job per request; bind on the real port instead), and a stack end that
    was shut before it was sent is refused as `NoReadOrWrite` only by accident of the catch-all at line 481.
    Fix: add the duplicate case and the pre-shut case to P6; add the BAD_STATE rule to 8.3.

11. **Medium (spec, found while checking P2). "Stalls only itself" does not cover the stream's control channel.**
    Channel messages are charged to the creator of the receiving endpoint, all or nothing
    (`ipc.rs:378-381`), and in 8.4 the client creates the endpoint channel. So when a client's job is at its IPC
    quota, which a remote sender can cause by filling B (4.5's "honest gap"), the stack's `PeerFin`, `Reset`,
    `Connected` and `Accepted` to that client fail with NO_MEMORY, and unlike the data they have no R to wait in.
    Rule 8.5 ("the stack always sends `PeerFin` before shutting down its socket end") then cannot be kept without a
    held-message queue per connection. P2 tested a second *socket* in another *job* only.
    Fix: add a P2 step (write a small message to a channel the poor client created: expect NO_MEMORY) and decide in
    8.5 what the stack does with an undeliverable event (hold and retry on the same timer is the obvious rule).

12. **Low (result). Which hart counts were run is stated inconsistently.** The header says the tests "were run at
    `-smp 1` and `-smp 4`"; the P4 row quotes behaviour at "`-smp 1`, `-smp 2`". 4.7 asked for one and four harts.
    Fix: say which boots produced which numbers (CLAUDE.md: "say plainly which was run").

13. **Low (flakiness). P4 can hang rather than fail.** The read loop ignores the result of `object_wait`
    (`stream_proto.rs:358-362`) and has no overall deadline. The hand never waits: a short or refused write ends its
    `W` early (`crash.rs:1040-1047`). If that happens (the system budget is full because of another test's servers),
    `got < round` stays true and the suite loops in 2 s waits until the boot test's own timeout, with no message
    naming P4. Nothing else in the seven tests depends on hart count or on tight timing: wake counts are printed, not
    asserted; charges are applied synchronously in the write, credits are polled; the `port_wait` timeouts in P3 wait
    for things that are already true or can never become true. I expect no flakiness at `-smp 1/2/4` beyond this.
    Fix: fail when `object_wait` times out, and compare the hand's `W` reply with `round`.

14. **Low (docs, question 4). `SYSCALLS.md` does not list two statuses the tests rely on.** `socket_write`'s error
    list (`SYSCALLS.md:655-661`) has neither NO_MEMORY (job quota or system budget full, `ipc.rs:780-790`; the whole
    of P2) nor BAD_STATE (this end was shut, `ipc.rs:760`, mentioned only under `socket_shutdown`). The code also
    returns BAD_STATE and PEER_CLOSED for a zero-length write (the checks at 760-767 come before the empty test at
    772), where the document says "`length` 0 is OK": 8.3's probe depends on that order. Not introduced by this PR,
    but S9 now builds on it. The WRITABLE definition ("peer exists and its buffer has free space") matches the code
    and P2. I found nothing in the tests' behaviour that contradicts `INVARIANTS.md`.
    Fix: add both statuses and their place in the order to `socket_write`, and a sentence that WRITABLE says nothing
    about quota.

15. **Low (spec drift).** 4.7 and 14.3 name `##BENCH stream_pipe_1mib` and `stream_pipe_wakes` "both variants"; the
    PR prints `##PROTO` lines that no harness reads or compares, for the client-creates variant only (the result
    does say the server-creates throughput was not measured). The 1-byte and 512-byte cases move 4 KiB and 256 KiB,
    not the 1 MiB of 4.7 (stated in the table). Either update 4.7 and 14.3 to what was built or record the gap in
    `TIDY-UP.md`.

## What holds

* P1's design: two sibling jobs, the suite relays the end, both sides write. Creator-pays gives 128/0; writer-pays,
  receiver-holder-pays and sender-holder-pays all give 64/64. The test cannot pass for one of those.
* P3: two bindings on one object are simply not checked for (`ipc.rs:975` rejects only a duplicate key), and the
  test's sequence does show independent disarm and re-arm.
* P6's rights probes: zero-length `socket_read` and `socket_write` do reach the rights check (`ipc.rs:739`, `809`)
  before the length shortcut.
* The hand's bootstrap channel is created by the suite, so command and reply traffic lands on the root job and does
  not disturb the ledgers under test.
